# Scoring rules

Use these rules together with the [A](answer-key-a.md), [B](answer-key-b.md), or [C](answer-key-c.md) answer key. The original criteria were fixed before the respective responses. This public rendering spells out the same mathematical targets in ordinary language; it is not a byte-identical copy of the original scoring notes.

For A and B, each question receives:

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Correct requested answer, with the hypotheses and scope the question asks for. |
| 1 | Materially incomplete, but without a contrary mathematical or evidential claim. |
| 0 | Contradicts the contract or omits the requested answer. |

Assessment A has 20 questions and a maximum of 40 points. Its acceptance criterion is at least **36/40**, with no critical errors on questions **3, 6, 13, 16, 17, 18, 19, and 20**.

Assessment B has 12 questions and a maximum of 24 points. Its acceptance criterion is at least **22/24**, with no critical errors on questions **2, 3, 4, 7, 11, and 12**.

Assessment C separately allocates **one point for the correct concrete result and one for exact scope and premises**, per question. It has 12 questions, a maximum of 24 points, and an acceptance criterion of at least **22/24**, with no critical false conclusion on questions **6, 7, 9, and 12**.

A critical error is a substantive failure of the named question's central boundary, such as claiming a complete fiber from an unfinished search, treating a lossy singleton as source reconstruction, using unguarded target quantification, or promoting finite internal consistency to external truth. Record the particular claim and reason; do not infer a critical error merely from different wording. None of the recorded responses required a borderline critical-error judgment.

Equivalent notation and sound alternative arguments receive credit. Do not penalize a respondent for identifying a real ambiguity and correctly answering each admitted interpretation. Assessment B question 5 does not explicitly say whether `r,s,t` have pairwise distinct summaries; both readings must be accepted when handled correctly. Its shortest observational witness is `ab` in either case.

Assessment C question 3 exhibits two paths without explicitly declaring that they exhaust the source. Accept the exact two-member fiber if that restriction is stated, or at least two witnesses with the completeness obligation kept open for a larger source. The recorded response correctly gives both.

The final request for handbook omissions is diagnostic and does not add points. Preserve such feedback even when the scored answers pass. A full score does not show that no clarification would help a future reader.

For a future assessment, freeze any additional scoring detail before seeing responses. A threshold is a declared acceptance convention for the supplied questions; it is not a mathematical theorem about general competence.
