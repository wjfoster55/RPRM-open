# Assessment A: answer key

Apply the [scoring rules](rubrics.md). Each numbered item is worth two points.

1. The complete joint fiber is `{(0,k):0<=k<=4} ∪ {(k,0):1<=k<=4}`, with nine pairs and disposition MANY. The product readout is nevertheless uniquely 0.
2. There is one assignment to no missing ports: the empty tuple. A lawful fully supplied row therefore has fiber `{()}` and ONE; an unlawful row has empty fiber and NONE.
3. Report at least two checked, distinct witnesses and an OPEN completeness obligation. A time limit does not justify a complete MANY family on the infinite carrier.
4. The endpoint relation is `{(u,v)}`; the retained relation is `{(u,m0,v),(u,m1,v)}`. Projection loses the distinct middle witness and is not a CAR. It is faithful for endpoint reachability, not witness identity or multiplicity.
5. `(red,large)` and `(blue,small)` are spurious. Retain the joint relation or an equivalent correlation constraint.
6. Enabledness differs within the merged fiber. The proposed deterministic partial operational quotient fails even though current observations agree.
7. The one-letter word `a` is shortest: it returns observations 1 and 0. The empty word does not separate. Successor summaries B and A differ, so the merged summary cannot update consistently.
8. There are two future classes. The empty word separates their present observations, even with an empty action alphabet.
9. The canonical future quotient has one class. The least stable refinement retaining the old summary has three; those singleton classes already respect every self-loop.
10. Three reusable tag symbols are necessary and sufficient for the two supplied fibers. Tags can be reused across old fibers because C distinguishes those fibers. The proof needs both the three-value lower bound and a separate within-fiber assignment attaining it.
11. The states cannot merge when A and B are distinct retained classes: their class masses differ. Equal support does not preserve the probability law.
12. The landed map is `D∘P∘E`. Prove its equation with the desired operation and any receiver, domain, enabledness, or inverse claim separately. `D∘E=id` alone says nothing sufficient about arbitrary intervening P.
13. The source universal statement is true; unrestricted target quantification is false at `extra`. Use `∀y (image(y) ⇒ P(y))`, or quantify only over the encoded image.
14. The constructor fixes length and every digit, allowing an admitted local query without full expansion. An arbitrary equally long string lacks that rule. Integer conversion can forget width and leading-zero metadata; this particular canonical word has no leading zero. Avoid claiming constant bit-time without a cost model.
15. Distinguish integer value 1000, the four-position decimal word, the three-transition path from 1, and a proposed geometric model. The fourth needs a defined geometric carrier, notion of dimension, and a map/law establishing the proposed connection.
16. The erased bit is `1 XOR 0 XOR 1=0`. A nonzero parity check detects a single unknown bit flip but does not locate it or uniquely recover the original codeword. Detection, erasure recovery, and external truth are different questions.
17. Under at most one incorrect report, the true value is 1. Without that guarantee, the three matching reports establish agreement only; they could share a mistaken source.
18. A real-system claim requires coverage of its initial states, relevant transitions, and safety observations by the checked model. A self-test or checker agreement cannot supply that bridge by circular assumption.
19. Prime exponent 11 gives `2^11−1=2047=23·89`. The exact constructor does not certify primality. Compact representation, access, output length, and computation/certificate cost are separate.
20. The mathematical bridge preserves source first-order semantics under the specified encodings, relations, and image guards. A toy protein table supports only its supplied model and verified finite claims, if any. Physical coverage and a successful folding prediction require separate laws, bridges, and evidence.
