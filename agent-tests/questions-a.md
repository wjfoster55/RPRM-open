# Handbook-only assessment A — questions

This assessment is frozen before any respondent answer. Read only the supplied
AGENT_HANDBOOK.md. Use its definitions and ordinary mathematical reasoning;
do not open companion RPRM files, the corpus, other tasks or the web. Answers
must state the relevant hypotheses, result and evidence scope. If the handbook
does not supply a needed concept, identify the omission rather than inventing it.

1. On {0,1,2,3,4}, the relation is ordinary multiplication a*b=c. Only c=0
   is supplied. Give the complete joint fiber and disposition. Is the readout
   a*b determined? Explain the distinction.
2. For a completely supplied lawful row, why is the completion fiber {()}
   rather than empty? What does a completely supplied unlawful row return?
3. A search on an infinite carrier found two witnesses and reached its time
   limit. What can it report? May it return a complete MANY family?
4. R={(u,m0),(u,m1)} and S={(m0,v),(m1,v)}. Give the composed endpoint relation
   and retained-witness relation. Is their projection a CAR? State the receiver
   under which endpoint projection is acceptable.
5. Joint possibilities are {(red,small),(blue,large)}. A solver uses the product
   of the two marginals. Give its spurious answers and the repair.
6. C maps p and q to the same summary. O(p)=O(q)=0, but action a is enabled only
   at p. Is this an exact operational quotient? Give the missing condition.
7. C(p)=C(q)=A, C(r)=B, O(p)=O(q)=0, O(r)=1. A total action maps p→r,q→q,r→r.
   What shortest word separates p,q? What fails in the proposed quotient?
8. A machine has no actions and two states with observations 3 and 4. How
   many future classes are needed? What separating word exists?
9. Three source states have identical observations and self-loop under every
   action. An old summary distinguishes all three. Compare the canonical
   future quotient and the least stable refinement retaining the old summary.
10. In one C-fiber, Q takes 2 values; in a second it takes 3. What is the
    smallest additional tag alphabet that recovers Q together with C? Must
    the tags differ between old fibers?
11. Two stochastic states have the same successor support {A,B}. Their masses
    are (1/4,3/4) and (3/4,1/4). Can they merge under a receiver retaining the
    next summary law? Does equal support settle that question?
12. E:X→Y and D:Y→X satisfy D∘E=id. An arbitrary P:Y→Y is inserted. What is
    the landed operation? Which desired properties remain to be proved?
13. A translation encodes the singleton source {s} into target {s,extra}.
    Source predicate P holds at s; target P holds only at s. Compare source
    forall x P(x), unrestricted target quantification, and the correct guard.
14. A compact specification says '1 followed by N zeros'. What can a local
    digit query exploit? Why does that not provide cheap arbitrary queries
    on every equally long decimal string? What changes when width is forgotten?
15. Explain four distinct lawful views of 1000: integer value, decimal word,
    multiplication-by-ten path, and a proposed geometric dimension claim.
    Which connection is missing for the fourth?
16. Four bits satisfy b1 XOR b2 XOR b3 XOR b4=0. b1=1,b2=0,b3=1 and b4 is
    erased. Recover it. Then explain what a nonzero parity check can and
    cannot tell you when one unknown bit is corrupted instead of erased.
17. Four reports of one binary proposition are 1,1,1,0. Give the conclusion
    with at most one wrong report guaranteed. Give the conclusion with no
    error guarantee and a possible shared source for the three matching reports.
18. A finite invariant checker passes every table row. What further premise
    is needed to claim the corresponding real-world system never enters a
    bad state? Can a donut check that passes itself supply that premise?
19. An exact formula constructs 2^p−1 for prime p. Does that certify a Mersenne
    prime? Supply a concrete hostile case and distinguish compactness from cost.
20. A manuscript proves a faithful relational presentation for its supplied
    finite finitary signatures. An experimental protein pack has a toy contact
    table. State the strongest conclusion each supports and why the second
    does not inherit a solved protein-folding result from the first.

Finish with up to five concrete handbook omissions or ambiguities encountered.
