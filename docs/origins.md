# Initial states, generative models, and origins

A relational model can explain how a specified construction begins: declare
its starting state, the rules that can act on it, and the observations that
matter. This makes assumptions about a beginning available for inspection.
A physical account of the universe's beginning asks for additional evidence.

The book develops specific models in
[III.8, return depth and black holes](../MANIFESTO.md#iii8-return-depth-exterior-observations-and-black-holes),
[III.9, two-path interference](../MANIFESTO.md#iii9-two-path-interference-and-retained-coherence),
and [III.10, gravity, magnetic motion and expansion](../MANIFESTO.md#iii10-gravity-magnetic-motion-and-cosmic-expansion).
Their supplied equations and written representation results do not establish
a new physical origin mechanism. In particular, the
[physical bridge for return depth](../MANIFESTO.md#the-physical-bridge-that-remains-open)
remains open. The initial-state distinctions below apply before such a bridge
has been supplied.

## What a model starts with

One precise finite setup is

`M = (S, I, Sigma, T, O, ~)`.

S is the state carrier; I is the admitted initial-state set; Sigma names the
operations; T gives their transitions or enabledness; O is the observation;
and `~` is an explicitly justified observational equivalence. If a grammar
constructs states, specify its seeds and formation rules as well. A carrier
does not supply a preferred initial state, transition, clock, probability,
geometry, or observer by itself.

Two distinct questions are then available:

1. What follows from the supplied initial conditions and rules?
2. Which initial conditions or rules satisfy specified observations?

The first is a forward evolution or deduction. The second is an inverse fiber
over a declared hypothesis carrier. It can be NONE, ONE, or MANY. A unique
member of a selected finite list is unique in that list; identifying an actual
source additionally requires that the source belongs to the list.

The model in [the proof-donut example](proof-donut.md) fixes four states,
two cycles, and initial state 0. Its future is forced under its stated rule.
Changing the rule or allowing another initial state changes the question.
The original choice of rule and seed remains a premise of that model.

## What a zero can mean

An arithmetic zero, a balanced readout, an empty carrier, an absent
measurement, a seed label, and a hidden state are different objects.

For exact rational lanes constrained by `S+R=1`, let `z=S-R`. Then

```text
(S,R) = (0,1)       -> z = -1
(S,R) = (1/2,1/2)   -> z =  0
(S,R) = (1,0)       -> z = +1

S = (1+z)/2, R = (1-z)/2.
```

The inverse reconstructs the pair because the sum-one law was retained.
The balanced readout zero has two specified lanes behind it. It is not an
empty set or unmeasured value. For k positive equal lanes of unit total,
balance is 1/k per lane; one-half belongs to the two-lane case. The k=0 case
cannot use this formula.

Likewise, a finite state named 0 can seed a cycle under a supplied update.
The name provides neither the update nor a physical act of creation. An
interface exposing no internal coordinates can refer to an existing complete
state; opacity alone establishes no closure or preservation theorem.

## Continuation from finite structure

A finite permutation T with `T^m(s)=s`, m>=1, defines an indefinitely
repeatable mathematical orbit by induction. Each state remains in a finite
carrier. An implementation storing a fresh copy of every historical step
uses a growing history carrier, which is a different storage question.

An attachment grammar can also construct successively larger objects, but
its occurrence types, compatible boundaries, construction rules and closure
conditions must be supplied. Equal counts or similar pictures do not derive
an adapter between a finite cycle, a growing graph, and a spatial universe.

Finite claims can be exact while questions about extending the carrier remain
open. Such a model is usable now: test its inverses, complete fibers,
conservation laws and distinct observable futures at the declared bounds.
Each proved extension states what permits the next stage.

## The physical origins aperture remains open

To use this language for a physical origin model, supply a concrete bridge:

| Required ingredient | The question it must answer |
|---|---|
| Physical interpretation | Which mathematical objects represent measurable matter, fields, geometry, or other observables? |
| Evolution and initial conditions | Which dynamics are defined, where do they apply, and which initial assumptions remain necessary? |
| Derived constraints | Which conservation, causal, or geometric relations follow from those specified rules? |
| Empirical separation | Which measurement distinguishes the proposed model from competing descriptions? |

These are open modeling obligations, not consequences of a coordinate shift
or a finite verifier returning PASS. No physical cosmology is asserted here.
The mathematical contribution is a language for making a beginning's
assumptions, alternatives, and consequences explicit, together with finite
tools for checking the parts that have actually been specified.
