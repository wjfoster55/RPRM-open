# Executive summary

RPRM proposes that reality may be built from relational states and changes in relational states rather than from finished independent objects. A potential relation becomes selectively coupled, an activation condition makes that coupling consequential, and a **registration** changes the state of participants, the relation, the topology, or an effective transition rule. **Retention** is the resulting path dependence: later events begin from a changed relational situation. The relational configuration may also carry **pressure, load, strain, deficit, or excitability**, so the substrate is not only a passive graph of possible interactions.

The strongest post-v3.1 development is a two-operation architecture:

> **Bind / continue:** relations form mutually supporting closures that preserve an organization.  
> **Reconfigure / snap:** accumulated load, lost correction margin, or self-recruiting activation drives a threshold transition that redistributes capacity, changes topology, divides a closure, propagates a cascade, or creates a new closure.

A temporary ontology has emerged around **relatrons**, primitive relational roles or degrees of freedom, and **relational units (RUs)**, a provisional ledger quantity that may be free, bound into closures, or distributed through relations. Two initially undifferentiated roles may become a reciprocal dyad, `U + U → [A ⇄ B]`; finite capacity, compatibility, saturation, and retention may then force chains, loops, branches, matter-like closures, and higher organization.

A major strong hypothesis is **scale recursion**:

> A stable closure formed at one level can become one effective relatron at the next level, where the same grammar of coupling, registration, retention, threshold, and reconfiguration acts again.

Under this view, a large event can be composed of smaller events of the same transition class. Complexity is not merely more events; it is more nested levels of controlled thresholds, retained alternatives, and relational leverage.

The model now contains explicit emergence branches for matter, solidity, inertia, gravity, electromagnetism, phase, black holes, cell division, life, desire, identity, artificial minds, and cosmology. These branches are not separate fundamental forces stapled onto the kernel. The research goal is to determine whether one unchanged local rule can generate them as different states, modes, scales, or solutions.

The mathematical branches include triadic closure and branching, powers-of-two state growth, four-role-to-three-mode projection, quaternionic and spinorial rotation, Hopf-style hidden-phase readout, recursive four-block tiers, Mersenne active-state counts, prime irreducibility, effective dimension, and dimensional overflow. These are live formal leads, not a numerological conclusion.

The current admitted Aetherwell result does **not** establish relation-borne memory, interference robustness, or physical evidence. It establishes that a deterministic relation-state toy law can retain a programmed scalar above a programmed readability threshold for a tested horizon. Quiet and “interference” traces were numerically identical through their shared 32-step horizon. The only justified immediate successor is the pinned `MATCHED-HORIZON-64-01 v1` audit of `Wide 0879 R`; carrier-versus-history localization requires new independently manipulable state registers and interventions.

# Part I — Minimal kernel

## 1. Relational primacy [CORE]

Reality may be fundamentally constituted by relations, relational roles, and changes in relational structure rather than by objects whose complete properties exist independently of all interaction.

This does not require that familiar objects are unreal. It proposes that familiar objects are **retained higher-order organizations** of a deeper relational substrate.

The shortest statement is:

> A distinction becomes consequential through relation; the interaction changes the relational situation; the changed situation constrains what can happen next.

## 2. Selective causal relevance [CORE]

Not every difference matters to every other state. A substrate state has **susceptibility** to some distinctions, and a **coupling** specifies:

- which distinction can matter;
- to which role or relation;
- with what strength;
- in what orientation or transformation;
- under what conditions.

This selective structure is the non-conscious descendant of the early word “attention.” That term remains retired.

## 3. Activation and registration [CORE / OPEN]

A possible coupling is not automatically an event.

**Activation** is the unresolved condition that makes a possible relation fire. Candidate forms include:

- a propagating excitation;
- a state gradient;
- an accumulated deficit or strain;
- a critical threshold;
- an asymmetric source–receiver condition;
- available free capacity;
- stochastic perturbation;
- or a change in another relation.

When activation makes a coupling consequential, a **registration** occurs.

Registration is an actual physical state transition. It may alter:

- participant states;
- the relation itself;
- topology;
- distributed substrate state;
- or effective transition parameters.

Measurement is one kind of registration. Consciousness is not required.

## 4. Retention as path dependence [CORE]

Retention means the post-registration system is not relationally identical to the pre-registration system.

Four levels remain distinct:

1. **State retention:** a participant changed.
2. **Relation retention:** a coupling changed.
3. **Topology retention:** possible future relations changed.
4. **Effective-rule retention:** parameters or protocols governing future changes changed under a deeper meta-rule.

Retention is not necessarily a readable record. It is whatever makes two systems with the same coarse visible state behave differently because they arrived there through different histories.

## 5. Relational disequilibrium [CORE]

The relational state may contain a dynamical disequilibrium rather than merely a passive map.

The umbrella term **relational pressure** currently covers several candidates:

- **deficit:** insufficient viable relation;
- **strain:** an existing relation displaced from a retained condition;
- **mismatch:** two demanded relational results fail to reconcile;
- **load:** unresolved state that must be absorbed or redistributed;
- **excitability:** closeness to a self-recruiting transition.

These may be separate variables, regimes of one variable, or partly redundant descriptions. v3.3 does not force them into one scalar.

## 6. Observer independence [CORE]

The same relational mechanism applies whether or not a person knows, sees, or records the event. An unattended detector, field, photon, chemical reaction, cell, or computational process can participate in registration.

## 7. Minimal process [CORE]

The smallest current grammar is:

```text
susceptibility
→ selective coupling
→ activation
→ registration
→ retention
→ changed pressure / compatibility / availability
→ later registration
```

The model still owes an exact state space and local update law.

# Part II — The new working architecture

## 8. Two complementary operations: bind and reconfigure [STRONG]

The dyad-focused versions of RPRM described persistence better than change. Post-v3.1 work exposed a complementary operation.

### Bind / continue

Relations become mutually supporting enough to recreate the same organization.

This produces:

- dyads;
- closures;
- retained identity;
- rewrite resistance;
- stable matter-like organization.

### Reconfigure / snap

The present organization loses correction margin, exceeds local capacity, recruits a cascade, or becomes cheaper to partition or rewrite than to preserve.

This produces:

- discharge;
- propagation;
- phase transition;
- division;
- collapse;
- avalanche;
- reset;
- a new closure.

The full grammar is therefore:

```text
bind
→ retain
→ accumulate / recruit / strain
→ reach critical margin
→ reconfigure
→ redistribute
→ recover
→ bind again
```

A “snapback” is the large-scale end of this family, not the name of every elementary state change.

## 9. Relatrons and relational units [STRONG / OPEN]

### Relatron

A temporary name for a primitive relational participant, role, degree of freedom, or addressable locus.

A relatron is not assumed to be a tiny ball in pre-existing space. The term exists so the model can refer to the participants before their ontology is settled.

### Relational unit (RU)

A temporary measurement name for a quantity in the relational ledger.

A toy implementation may define one RU operationally, for example as the normalized capacity required to sustain a baseline reciprocal relation for one update. Nature is not assumed to use this unit.

Candidate forms are:

```text
free RU
bound RU
distributed / field RU
stored strain RU
active / propagating RU
```

A candidate conservation law is:

```text
total RU
=
free + bound + distributed + stored + active
```

This is not yet canonical. The key insight is accounting: if neighboring states gain something because of a center, some ledger entry must decrease, convert, or be supplied.

## 10. Dyadic differentiation [STRONG]

The simplest stable relation may arise from two initially undifferentiated roles:

```text
U + U → [A ⇄ B]
```

A and B need not be pre-existing substances. Their distinction may be created by the relationship.

The dyad may be:

- two participants;
- one relational object with two roles;
- two internal roles at one external address;
- or a coarse description of a richer closure.

Its defining feature is reciprocal constraint: A cannot change independently of the A–B relation, and B cannot change independently of it.

## 11. Saturation, compatibility, and relational security [STRONG]

A dyad cannot accept arbitrary additional roles without limit.

Candidate constraints include:

- finite local relational capacity;
- one-to-one role requirements;
- incompatible simultaneous closures;
- overloaded activation;
- topology limits;
- phase or orientation mismatch;
- exclusion-like state constraints.

A retained closure has **relational security**: its current organization is supported by enough compatible relations that leaving requires a coordinated rewrite.

The rule is not “always move toward a larger number.” It is closer to:

> Preserve a viable established closure unless another continuation improves viability enough to pay the full rewrite cost of leaving.

## 12. Matter as bound organization [STRONG]

Matter is not used as the bottom-level explanation.

A matter-like object is a cross-supported retained organization of relational capacity. Its identity depends on topology and dynamic closure, not merely on total RU count.

This creates a candidate distinction:

```text
same RU quantity
≠
same object
```

Organization is part of state.

## 13. Most self-consistent continuation [STRONG / OPEN]

A closure has several locally available next states.

The current candidate principle is:

> The realized continuation is the dynamically available state that preserves viable closure while requiring the least incompatible relational rewrite.

This is a placeholder for an eventual exact functional, not a license to explain any result after the fact.

Candidate terms in the functional include:

- internal closure preservation;
- retained direction;
- local support;
- mismatch relief;
- saturation and compatibility;
- activation cost;
- topology rewrite cost;
- external registration consequences.

## 14. Critical margin [STRONG]

Every closure may have a finite ability to absorb relational demand while remaining the same organization.

Define conceptually:

```text
correction margin
=
capacity to reconcile change
− unresolved relational demand
```

Regimes:

```text
margin > 0:
disturbance can be absorbed

margin = 0:
one full correction no longer makes net progress

margin < 0:
preserving the old closure fails; reconfiguration grows
```

This is the cleanest current interpretation of the “divide-by-zero” or tipping-point intuition: not literal arithmetic failure, but the denominator-like quantity “progress per correction” reaching zero.

## 15. Cascade gain [STRONG]

A local transition exports change into neighboring relations.

Define conceptually:

```text
cascade gain
=
expected successor transitions
per initiating transition
```

Regimes:

```text
gain < 1:
activity dies

gain = 1:
activity reproduces itself at the margin

gain > 1:
activity expands
```

This connects:

- the five-to-four grid;
- Pandemic-style overflow;
- orgasm-like recruitment;
- neural firing;
- fracture avalanches;
- ring recursion;
- cell division cascades;
- and speculative snapback.

The shared number need not be one raw threshold across every domain. The invariant may be the relationship “output reproduces one successor event.”

## 16. Threshold redistribution cycle [STRONG]

The repeated “four-five” and Pandemic imagery produced a candidate local operation:

```text
stable occupancy
→ one more unit arrives
→ local capacity is exceeded
→ some quantity is redistributed
→ source returns to a stable regime
→ neighbors approach or cross their thresholds
```

The exact primitive propagation remains open:

- one intact token moves;
- a quantity divides among neighbors;
- a field relaxes continuously;
- or a topology change redistributes constraints.

The important distinction is:

> Every snapback is made from registrations; not every registration is a snapback.

## 17. Scale-recursive relational dynamics [STRONG]

This is the strongest post-v3.1 architecture statement.

> A stable lower-level closure can be treated as one effective relatron at the next level, and the same relational grammar may act among those effective units.

The hierarchy is:

```text
relatrons
→ dyads
→ closures
→ chemical organizations
→ cells
→ organisms
→ nervous systems
→ societies / machines
```

At every scale the candidate grammar is:

```text
couple
→ register
→ retain
→ approach threshold
→ recruit / reconfigure
→ create a new closure
```

This is the precise form of:

> **The things created here are the relatrons of the next thing.**

## 18. Transition universality [STRONG]

Very different domain mechanisms may share one state-transition grammar:

```text
stable organization
→ slow preparation
→ critical margin
→ local trigger
→ recruitment
→ rapid reconfiguration
→ recovery / refractory state
→ retained alteration
```

Two interpretations remain live:

- one shared substrate mechanism expressed through different materials;
- different microscopic mechanisms flowing into the same universality class.

RPRM’s strong hypothesis is that the first is true at the deepest level and the second describes higher-level effective behavior.

## 19. Nested snapback [STRONG / BRANCH]

Large transitions may be built from smaller transitions of the same class.

```text
local registration
→ micro threshold crossing
→ module transition
→ higher-level recruitment
→ macro transition
```

A toe movement, cell division, climax, fracture, and cosmic reset are not asserted to be literally identical. They may be nested implementations of one relational event grammar.

## 20. Configuration capacity [STRONG / OPEN]

Two systems with the same RU quantity can differ in how many structured states they can:

- distinguish;
- retain;
- access;
- transition among;
- and use to trigger other states.

This is **configuration capacity**.

Complexity is therefore not only more relatrons or more events. It is more nested conditional states, gates, thresholds, and controlled cascades.

## 21. Relational leverage [STRONG]

Relational leverage is:

> coordinated retained state change produced per unit of external triggering change.

A highly organized system can prepare a large cascade so that a small final registration selects or releases it.

Biology, brains, computers, and learned models may be especially revealing because they explicitly control high relational leverage.

## 22. Reflexive recapitulation [BRANCH]

As closures become more complex, some acquire the ability to model and manipulate the same state-transition grammar from which they are built.

```text
relational dynamics
→ life
→ nervous systems
→ internal models
→ computation
→ learned relational models
→ reverse engineering of relational dynamics
```

This is not universal intention. It is the generative process becoming explicit inside one of its own products.

# Part III — Emergence branches

## 23. Solidity and exclusion [BRANCH]

The benchmark fact “matter prevents matter from occupying the same ordinary state” is not accepted as the deepest explanation.

The RPRM branch is:

> Two entrenched closures cannot be superposed locally without either sharing compatible roles or paying a large coordinated rewrite cost.

Candidate ingredients:

```text
finite local capacity
+ saturation
+ incompatible closure assignments
+ cross-support
+ retention
= solidity / exclusion-like resistance
```

This produces a regime map rather than absolute impenetrability:

- weak contact: stable support relation;
- moderate input: elastic deformation;
- stronger input: plastic rewrite;
- still stronger input: fracture or penetration;
- released mismatch: heat, vibration, sound, or topology change.

A successful model must eventually reproduce the established quantum and electromagnetic benchmarks underlying matter stability rather than hide them behind an occupancy flag.

## 24. Motion and inertia [BRANCH]

Motion may be propagation of a retained organization rather than a bead moving through an independent container.

A stable object’s next state is easiest along the continuation it has already been realizing. Changing that direction requires rewriting a coordinated sequence of relations.

Candidate interpretation:

> **Inertia is retention of continuation.**

The same substrate may implement translation by strengthening relations ahead and releasing them behind, preserving organizational continuity without preserving every primitive participant.

## 25. Gravity as emergent continuation state [STRONG BRANCH]

Gravity is not identified with one shape or one dyad. It is an aggregate state generated by the common, externally undirected component of many stable reciprocal closures.

Candidate chain:

```text
bound closures
→ local substrate reorganization
→ neighbor-to-neighbor propagation
→ distributed continuation / support field
→ asymmetric viability of local next states
→ apparent attraction
```

A target does not need to know the distant source. It registers only the local relational state.

### Conserved support picture

A high local source may redistribute relational capacity into neighboring locations. The field is not copied from nothing.

### All-eights picture

Closures surrounded by compatible support expose less mismatch or boundary burden than isolated closures. Nearby aggregates may therefore be easier to maintain.

### Continuation geometry picture

The strongest branch is that sources alter which local continuations exist or are least costly, so paths bend without a literal pull.

These remain discriminable alternatives:

1. direct mutual reinforcement;
2. shared maintenance burden;
3. emergent continuation geometry.

## 26. Gravity–inertia equivalence [STRONG TARGET]

One bound relational quantity may perform three jobs:

1. source the gravity-like continuation state;
2. determine the object’s response to that state;
3. determine resistance to changing continuation.

If all three scale with the same bound amount, source/response and inertia may cancel in free fall.

This is the highest-value gravity derivation target because it could make universal free fall a consequence of one relational ledger rather than a separate rule.

## 27. Common, differential, and cyclic dyad modes [STRONG]

A reciprocal dyad may expose different aspects of one relation.

### Common mode

A and B contribute together as one externally undirected state.

Candidate manifestation:

- scalar aggregate;
- gravity-like universal response;
- bound magnitude.

### Differential mode

The distinction between A and B remains externally exposed.

Candidate manifestation:

- sign;
- polarity;
- orientation;
- electric- or magnet-like behavior.

### Cyclic mode

A and B update one another with ordering, delay, or phase.

Candidate manifestation:

- oscillation;
- circulation;
- waves;
- internal clocking;
- phase-sensitive interaction.

The shorthand is:

> Gravity hides the difference.  
> Polarity exposes the difference.  
> Phase circulates the difference.

This is a generative branch, not a claim that current force laws are already derived.

## 28. Electricity, magnetism, light, and plasma [BRANCH]

A maximum-strength RPRM mapping is:

- **charge-like state:** an exposed signed relational role;
- **electric current:** propagation of differentiated role through a network;
- **magnet-like state:** retained orientation associated with moving or circulating differentiation;
- **light-like state:** self-propagating coupling between differential and cyclic modes;
- **plasma-like phase:** mobile differentiated roles collectively reconfiguring rather than remaining locked into ordinary closures.

The benchmark burden includes gauge structure, Maxwell-like dynamics, charge conservation, attraction and repulsion, finite propagation, and quantum behavior.

## 29. Circulatory closure [STRONG BRANCH]

Rotation is not assumed to be literal primitive spinning.

A reciprocal relation with finite delay and retained continuation may enter a bounded cyclic state.

Possible regimes:

```text
coupling too weak
→ escape / dispersal

coupling too strong with no delay
→ static clamp / collapse

intermediate coupling + delay
→ circulation / orbit
```

Circulation offers a solution to:

> Continue changing without losing locality.

Straight continuation preserves motion but loses the group. Static clamping preserves the group but loses circulation. A cyclic closure may preserve both.

## 30. Rotating-frame effects as one state family [BRANCH]

Centrifugal, Coriolis, and Euler-like behavior are treated as possible different readouts of one rotating relational state.

- **centrifugal-like:** retained straight continuation viewed inside a continually turning closure;
- **Coriolis-like:** cross-coupling between a moving third state and the changing orientation of the closure;
- **Euler-like:** response to a changing rotation rate.

The key experimental target is whether one local rule produces the correct scaling families without force-specific code.

## 31. Phase and interference [STRONG BRANCH]

The empirical benchmark is path distinguishability, partial interference, reversibility, and amplitude-like cancellation.

The old pressure-discharge intuition remains one branch:

> registration relieves or redirects unresolved relational load.

The standard distinguishability structure remains a benchmark:

> different alternatives become differently coupled to the rest of the system.

The synthesis target is:

> differential coupling changes how structured, orientation-bearing relational contributions can recombine.

A successful native mechanism must support:

- signed or oriented contributions;
- continuous relative phase;
- cancellation;
- probability-preserving composition;
- distinguishability tradeoffs;
- exact undo;
- and eventually the Born rule and Bell correlations.

## 32. Ring with basepoint and closure seam [STRONG]

William’s “C” is primarily a ring or chain link whose joining point remains conceptually visible.

A healthy return is:

```text
A → full traversal → A
```

A defective return is:

```text
A → full traversal → A'
```

The seam is where departure and return are compared.

It need not be a physical cut. It is the relational read/write or consistency point.

## 33. Exact repeated mismatch [STRONG]

The key black-hole intuition is not necessarily growing error magnitude.

At the critical state, one full loop may return **exactly the unresolved condition that caused it**.

```text
mismatch magnitude = constant
recursive depth = 1, 2, 3, 4...
```

The visible address repeats. The complete state does not.

This produces:

- double-helicoid imagery;
- same position, deeper sheet;
- recursion without external completion;
- infinite delay and causal non-return as two views of one process.

## 34. Black hole as collective trapped phase [STRONG BRANCH]

A candidate sequence is:

```text

→ reduced correction margin
→ return loop stops reducing mismatch
→ recursive depth increases
→ neighboring continuations are recruited
→ collective region loses outwardly resolving paths
```

The candidate source may be one defect, but the black hole proper is a region-level phase:

> every locally outward attempt ultimately returns into the recursive interior rather than completing outside it.

The horizon is:

> the boundary where the best outward continuation becomes marginal and then unavailable.

The singularity-like branch is where the native state-transition rule ceases to supply a valid continuation, diverges, bifurcates, or requires new physics.

## 35. Boundary and information [BRANCH]

Internal recursive depth may be enormous while external distinguishability is limited by the interface through which the interior can register with the exterior.

Candidate shape:

> internal complexity scales with the closure; externally accessible distinction is bottlenecked by boundary relations.

This is a target for explaining area-like information scaling, not a current result.

## 36. Creation as generative reconfiguration [STRONG]

“Creation begets creation” means that formation processes across scales may instantiate shared transition classes.

A common shape is:

```text
one viable organization

```

The domain details differ. The transition grammar may recur.

## 37. Generative bifurcation [STRONG BRANCH]

Controlled division differs from fracture.

```text

one closure → nonviable pieces

generative bifurcation:
one closure → multiple viable descendant closures
```

Candidate trigger branches:

- one closure exceeds capacity;
- support becomes insufficient;
- two self-maintenance centers emerge;
- coordination cost exceeds partition cost;
- a copied identity kernel makes two continuations possible.

Cell division is a principal reverse-engineering target.

## 38. Life as controlled closure [BRANCH]

Life may be a closure that actively controls the conditions of its own continuation.

Candidate operations:

- boundary maintenance;
- selective throughput;
- repair;
- error correction;
- reproduction;
- adaptation of future thresholds.

The transition from persistence to life may occur when a closure not only survives but acts on the environment to preserve and reproduce the closure process itself.

## 39. Desire and agency [BRANCH]

A self-maintaining closure has a viable relational range.

Candidate ladder:

```text
actual deficit / mismatch
→ internal representation
→ predicted relief route
→ selective action
→ outcome registration
→ retained update
```

Desire is not assigned to elementary relations. It may emerge when a self-modeling closure represents its own relational deficits and possible continuations.

## 40. Identity and re-instantiation [BRANCH]

RPRM separates:

- material continuity;
- causal continuity;
- structural equivalence;
- historical lineage;
- personal identity.

A closure may be re-instantiated in different components. That supports a naturalized Ship-of-Theseus “yes” at the level of structural identity.

It does not automatically settle whether an interrupted perfect copy is the same person.

## 41. Artificial minds [BRANCH]

If cognition is a realizable relational closure rather than a carbon-only substance, artificial minds are possible in principle.

The live fork is:

- causal organization is sufficient;
- or particular fundamental physical dynamics are required beyond simulation.

A useful operational target includes persistent identity, self-maintenance, meaningful internal deficits, environmental action, repair, memory, and self-modeling—not merely fluent output.

# Part IV — Arity, number, spin, and dimension

## 42. One, two, three, and composition [STRONG / MATH]

A recurring intuition is:

```text
one
→ identity

two
→ relation / difference / reciprocity

three
→ mediation / closure / context / branching

four and beyond
→ composition of relations
```

Two distinct “three breakthroughs” are preserved:

1. **Three participants** permit the first ordinary cycle.
2. **Three connections at one role** permit the first branch or junction.

DNA is a useful reverse-engineering example: two backbone connections preserve continuity while a third attachment carries variable base information.

## 43. Triadic breakthrough [STRONG BRANCH]

Candidate statement:

> Dyads permit coupling and persistence. A third relational role permits a relation to be mediated, contextualized, branched, or closed into a circuit.

This may be the smallest step from simple persistence to information-bearing organization.

## 44. Powers of two and state-space multiplication [STRONG]

Repeated binary distinctions multiply possible joint states:

```text
1 binary distinction → 2 states
2 distinctions → 4 states
3 distinctions → 8 states
n distinctions → 2^n states
```

Complexity can grow without creating proportionally more relational capacity. The same ledger may support exponentially more configurations.

This is separate from physical population doubling.

## 45. Four-role hidden state and three difference modes [STRONG / MATH]

Four underlying values sharing one fixed total have three independent redistributions:

```text
4 roles
− 1 conserved common condition
=
3 differential freedoms
```

For four equally coupled roles, one common mode and three equivalent difference modes arise naturally.

Candidate statement:

> Four may be the minimal fully symmetric hidden relational frame, while three is the effective difference-space exposed after the common total is fixed.

## 46. Spin as conversion [STRONG / MATH]

Spin is treated as an operation on relational state rather than necessarily literal mechanical rotation.

Candidate operation:

```text
rich hidden state
→ norm / total constraint
→ continuous orientation transformation
→ lower-dimensional or equivalence-class readout
```

Useful distinctions:

- transformation: any state map;
- rotation: norm-preserving orientation change;
- projection: lower-dimensional readout;
- quotient/identification: multiple hidden states count as the same visible state;
- conjugation: transformation by an operator and its inverse.

## 47. Four-to-three spin projection [STRONG BRANCH]

The strongest current interpretation of:

```text
one → two → four → spin → three
```

is:

> One-to-two and two-to-four enrich the hidden representation. Spin is the operation. Four-to-three is constrained readout rather than deletion.

Quaternionic rotation is an exact mathematical neighbor:

- four real components encode a unit orientation operator;
- one norm constraint leaves three degrees of freedom;

This aligns with:

A normalized four-component state can map to a three-component visible direction while retaining a hidden circular degree of freedom.

This suggests:

This is a formal neighbor for the GPT-to-spin intuition: rich hidden state, transformation, projection, and unobserved equivalence.
