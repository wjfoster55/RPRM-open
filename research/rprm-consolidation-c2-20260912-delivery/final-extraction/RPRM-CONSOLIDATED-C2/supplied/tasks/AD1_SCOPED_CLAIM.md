## Required small development sequence

Use one problem family and a compact, independently evaluated set of cases. The historical examples are exposed development material.

1. **Valid repeat:** rows `(P,5),(Q,8)` give SUM=SUM(DISTINCT)=13 for that instance. An unchanged requested result can be reused without pretending to establish a universal identity. Report any actual source access or work that validates reuse.
2. **Harmless transport:** reorder rows or consistently relabel a declared irrelevant ID. The mathematical answer remains valid under the same assumptions. Do not turn every changed byte sequence into mathematical invalidity; conservative cache policy and semantic necessity are different.
3. **Changed instance:** rows `(P,5),(Q,5)` give 10 versus 5. An instance-only equality claim must not survive this alteration unsupported. Recompute or use already sufficient retained evidence; do not require reopening when the retained information genuinely suffices.
4. **Changed quantifier:** the first equality does not authorize substitution on every admitted table. Produce the counterexample or a supported conditional statement, rather than laundering sample agreement into a theorem.
5. **Positive conditional reuse:** if the admitted schema/class genuinely ensures the relevant values are distinct, demonstrate why the equality rule survives source changes that preserve that assumption. Define duplicate, NULL and empty-input behavior explicitly, or exclude them by a declared admission rule. Do not overstate a sufficient condition as necessary in every instance.
6. **Evidence failure:** unavailable or altered backing must not produce an unsupported new answer. But an unrelated missing cold source need not invalidate a previously justified hot answer. An invalid source link is not proof that the mathematical possibility set is empty.
