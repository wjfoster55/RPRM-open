## Established literature used

1. J. Kogut and L. Susskind, *Hamiltonian formulation of Wilson's lattice
   gauge theories*, Physical Review D 11, 395 (1975).
   [Primary publisher record](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395).
   Supplies the established Hamiltonian lattice-gauge setting; the current
   graph and generator normalization are stated explicitly in this packet.
   Only the accessible publisher record/abstract was used here.
2. J. Baez, *Spin Network States in Gauge Theory*, especially Section 2,
   Lemma 3. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/9411007).
   Supplies completeness of the invariant fixed-graph spin-network
   decomposition. The all-spin gap argument uses that completeness, not
   finite support enumeration as a substitute for it.
3. M. Fürstenberg, G. Rudolph and M. Schmidt, *Defining relations for the
   orbit type strata of SU(2)-lattice gauge models*, Journal of Geometry
   and Physics 119 (2017), 66–81.
   [Primary preprint, especially Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1702.01047).
   Supplies the established maximal-tree/pointed-gauge reduction framework,
   residual conjugation, and moment-map setting. Their trivialization is
   converted to the right Maurer convention used here. The literature's
   invariance of phase-orbit strata is consistent with our witness: a
   configuration chart can fail while the full phase orbit is regular.
4. J. Huebschmann, G. Rudolph and M. Schmidt, *A gauge model for quantum
   mechanics on a stratified space*, Communications in Mathematical Physics
   286 (2009), 459–494.
   [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0702017), Sections 2 and 3,
   including the compact-group quantum Hamiltonian and invariant smooth
   core. Used for the source/operator setting, not as a proof that any
   arbitrary reduced-coordinate quantization agrees with the source.
5. T. Jakobs et al., [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02322v2),
   equation (42) in Section III.4 and equation (1).
   These reconcile the unit-three-sphere Casimir normalization with the
   `j(j+1)` convention and display the opposing electric/magnetic coupling
   factors. Numerical results or a finite spin truncation are not imported.
6. A. Jaffe and E. Witten, *Quantum Yang–Mills Theory*, especially
   Sections 4–6. [Official Clay problem statement](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf).
   Supplies the continuum quantum target and limit obligations. A positive
   finite-graph bound is not assigned those stronger conclusions.
