## 3. Bounded exact test: operation closure through order two

This is a proposed test contract for the new weak-coupling derivation. It
does not assert that the following normalization has already been matched
to every prior YM Hamiltonian.

**Carrier and types.** Fix one supplied finite graph, product Haar measure
`mu`, smooth real gauge-invariant functions, and pointwise equality on the
full continuous carrier. Fix the actual kinetic normalization by writing
`T=-sum_e a_e Delta_e`, with supplied positive `a_e`, and `H(r)=T+rV`.
Work in the finite jet ring `R[r]/(r^3)`: this bounds perturbative order,
not the continuous source configurations. Numerical samples do not prove
function equality. The coupling rescaling identifying this `r,V,T` with the
source Hamiltonian must be recorded.

**Supplied ports.** Exact `T`, `V`, Haar means, gauge action, and an explicit
finite function basis adequate for all order-two expressions. Supply an
exact inverse of `T` on each required mean-zero invariant span, or leave
that inversion OPEN. A checked finite matrix inverse is adequate when its
function-level invariance and equality identities have also been proved.

**Missing ports and direction.** Solve for the formal log-vacuum coefficients
`S(r)=r s1+r^2 s2`, with `<s1>_mu=<s2>_mu=0`, and the corresponding energy
coefficients. This choice fixes the irrelevant additive log normalization;
the associated density is normalized separately. It is a forward formal
construction from the supplied Hamiltonian, not an inverse claim about an
unknown physical source.

Define the kinetic gradient contraction by the exact product identity

```text
Gamma(f,g) = (f Tg + g Tf - T(fg))/2,
T(fg)=f Tg+g Tf-2 Gamma(f,g).
```

For this Laplacian convention, `T exp(S)/exp(S)=T S-Gamma(S,S)`.
Coefficient comparison in `H(r) exp(S)=E(r) exp(S)` therefore requires

```text

B2=Gamma(s1,s1),
E2=-<B2>,                 T s2=B2-<B2>.
```

These are formal written identities. Testing them for the actual source
means checking all of the following as full function identities:

1. The supplied basis, exact Haar means and kinetic matrix implement the

2. Every product needed through perturbative grade two, and every resulting

3. The computed `s1,s2,E1,E2` make the coefficients of `r` and `r^2` in

## 4. Why a finite grammar must declare its closure boundary

The binary character grammar benefits from a genuinely finite carrier:
`chi_u chi_v=chi_(u+v)` closes within the finite character family. That fact
cannot be imported unchanged to a connected continuous SU(2) carrier.

Here is a direct written obstruction. If a finite-dimensional unital real
subalgebra of `C(X)` is closed under ordinary multiplication, then for every
member `f`, the powers `1,f,f^2,...` are linearly dependent. A nonzero real
polynomial therefore annihilates `f` pointwise. Its image is contained in
the finite root set of that polynomial. On connected `X`, continuity makes
`f(X)` connected, hence a singleton. Every member is constant. Consequently
a finite linear family of nonconstant continuous readouts on connected
`SU(2)^E` cannot close under all products indefinitely.

A smallest hostile calculation is the normalized trace
`q(U)=Tr(U)/2` of one SU(2) holonomy. At `U=-I`, `diag(i,-i)`, and `I`, its
values are `-1,0,1`; `q^2` has values `1,0,1`. No affine function `a+bq`
has those three values. Thus `span{1,q}` fails product closure, even if a
separately supplied generator happens to preserve that span. A basis change
inside the same span cannot remove this defect.

This does not prohibit finite coordinates with nonlinear readout functions,
an infinite recursively generated algebra, or a finite-order graded
construction with proved error bounds. It requires declaring which of those
contracts is actually being used. The order-two test above adopts the graded
contract. Any growth in basis size and work remains part of its cost; a
short final expression does not remove construction or remainder costs.
