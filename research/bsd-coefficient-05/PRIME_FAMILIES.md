# The recovered prime-pattern atlas

The prior work did organize many families. Audit 102 declared a finite
carrier of all 1,229 primes through 10,000, bases2–16 and Gaussian
coordinates in [-32,32]². It recorded16 seed lenses and five adapters.
Audit 103 added12 overlays. These are attributed historical results; the
whole atlas has not been replayed in this experiment.

The closed object was that finite typed atlas and its tested operations,
not an exhaustive unbounded theory of every prime-like pattern.

| Family | What its membership means |
|---|---|
| Mersenne / Fermat primes | Prime values of 2^p−1 or2^(2^m)+1 under their stated exponent conditions |
| Lucky numbers / lucky primes | Survivors of a positional deletion sieve; lucky primes also pass an independent primality predicate |
| Superprimes | Primes whose index in the ordered prime sequence is itself prime |
| Emirps / circular primes | Decimal reversal or every cyclic digit rotation gives the required prime values; base and width matter |
| Chen / isolated neighbor views | A neighboring p+2 is prime or semiprime, or both p±2 are nonprime, inside the admitted coverage |
| Balanced / strong / weak prime windows | A consecutive-prime triple is classified by 2p−previous−next |
| Gaussian primes | Irreducibles in Z[i]; off-axis norm is a rational prime, while axis primes require the 3 mod 4 condition |
| Probable-prime tests / pseudoprimes | A specified congruence filters candidates; a composite that passes is a pseudoprime to that test. This differs from a genuine prime subclass. |

The atlas distinguishes candidate filters, post-prime classifiers, relation
views, proof receivers and intersections. A new label helps this BSD task
if it supplies a new operation or discriminating readout relevant to the
coefficient. Its name alone neither admits nor excludes it.

The successful route here uses an actual curve relation: E34 is a quadratic
twist of the small CM curve E1. That supplies a primitive quadratic
character, a Gauss sum, a much smaller exact modular-symbol calculation,
and an exact period conversion. It connects prime-derived Fourier data to
the coefficient through an explicit formula.

We also freshly counted E(F13) and E(F41), giving 20 and 52. Within the
same base prime5 construction, removing those Euler factors multiplies a
leading coefficient by 20/13 and 52/41. The first costs one absolute 5-adic
digit when divided out; the second is a 5-adic unit and preserves precision.
Both are useful calibration controls. Multiplying both sides by one known
factor leaves their unknown ratio unchanged, so it cannot substitute for
the independently normalized coefficient computed here.
