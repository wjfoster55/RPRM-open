# Two adjacent plaquette traces cannot carry the actual vacuum

12 September 2026. A new written obstruction at a fixed finite graph,
continuing [the conditional vacuum certificate](accepted/research/ym2_rail_closure/CONDITIONAL_RAIL.md)
and [the trial-reference equation](accepted/research/ym2_vacuum_handoff/TRIAL_REFERENCE.md).
No previous verifier is rerun. The result concerns full SU(2) link
variables with all vertex gauge constraints and no spin cutoff.

**Result.** At every nonzero coupling, the Hamiltonian on two elementary
squares sharing one edge has no nonzero eigenfunction that is a function
only of the two separate plaquette traces. This includes arbitrary joint
functions of those traces, not just products of one-plaquette factors.
The shared-edge kinetic term generates the six-edge outer-loop trace.

## 1. Contract and actual configuration fiber

The admitted graph consists of exactly two adjacent elementary squares,
with seven distinct links, six vertices, open boundary, and no boundary
charges. The configuration space is SU(2)^7 with product normalized Haar
measure. Equality of configurations is equality of their link values;
gauge equivalence is stated separately. The physical space is the
gauge-invariant subspace of L2. Keep the accepted unit-round SU(2)
metric and Hamiltonian

```text
T = -Delta/2,       A(r) = T-r(a+b),       r > 0.
```

The argument also holds for real r != 0. Each fundamental trace coordinate
on a single link has Laplacian eigenvalue -3, so an elementary four-link
plaquette has Ta=6a and Tb=6b.

Orient the common edge e from v0 to v1. Let A be the three-edge exterior
path from v1 back to v0 around the left square, and B the three-edge
exterior path from v0 to v1 around the right square. In this paragraph A
and B denote path holonomies, not the Hamiltonian A(r). Both plaquette
holonomies are based at v0:

```text
P=eA,       Q=Be^{-1},
a=Tr(P)/2,  b=Tr(Q)/2,  c=Tr(PQ)/2=Tr(AB)/2.
```

All three traces are gauge invariant. The last trace is the outer loop:
its expression after trace cyclicity contains exactly six exterior
links and excludes e. The two occurrences a and b remain distinct even
when their numerical values coincide.

Write SU(2) elements as unit quaternions P=(a,p), Q=(b,q). Then

```text
|p|^2=1-a^2,       |q|^2=1-b^2,       c=ab-p dot q.
```

Consequently the complete possible c fiber at supplied a,b in [-1,1] is

```text
I(a,b) = [ab-s, ab+s],       s=sqrt((1-a^2)(1-b^2)).    (CO1)
```

Every point of this interval is realized: choose vectors p,q with the
prescribed lengths and dot product ab-c. Take e=1; set one exterior A
link equal to P, one exterior B link equal to Q, and all other links to
1. This realizes the chosen pair of holonomies on the actual graph.
If s>0 the c fiber is MANY(I(a,b)); if s=0 it is ONE(ab). Input a or b
outside [-1,1] is an admission error. This is a complete fiber of the c
readout, not an enumeration of all link configurations.

The supplied representation is C(U)=(a,b). Its requested receiver is
the kinetic operation and then the pointwise local energy of a proposed
vacuum. The next sections show exactly why this representation loses
information needed by that receiver.

## 2. The cross gradient generated by the shared edge

Let X_1,X_2,X_3 be the orthonormal imaginary-quaternion basis of the
unit-round SU(2) tangent space. Vary only the shared edge by
e(t)=exp(tX_j)e. Then

```text
P(t)=exp(tX_j)P,       Q(t)=Q exp(-tX_j),
d a/dt|0 = -p_j,       d b/dt|0 = q_j.
```

The trace derivative follows directly from the scalar part of a
quaternion product. The two plaquettes share no other link, so their
full product-metric cross gradient is exactly

```text
Gamma(a,b) := sum_links grad_i a dot grad_i b
            = -p dot q = c-ab.                         (CO2)
```

For any one of the four links of a plaquette, its trace is the scalar
part of that link times fixed group elements. Left and right group
multiplication preserve the round metric; the squared gradient of a
scalar coordinate on the unit 3-sphere is 1-a^2. Summing the four links
therefore gives

```text
Gamma(a,a)=4(1-a^2),       Gamma(b,b)=4(1-b^2).          (CO3)
```

Equation CO2 is not determined by a and b on any fiber with s>0.
The required new joint quantity can be recorded either as c or as
Gamma(a,b)=c-ab; each determines the other when a,b are retained.
This is a six-edge Wilson-loop readout generated by a shared-edge
derivative, not an additional independently supplied field.

## 3. Exact failure of the additive log-vacuum class

Take a smooth positive candidate

```text
phi = exp(f(a)+g(b))/normalizer,
```

with enough regularity to define a smooth function on the link carrier.
Its conditional densities are positive, smooth, gauge covariant, and
compatible: they are conditionals of the displayed joint density
phi^2. Thus this class passes the path-consistency test CR2 exactly.

The local energy R=(A(r)phi)/phi follows from the product and chain rules:

```text
R(a,b,c) = U_f(a)+U_g(b)-f'(a)g'(b)(c-ab),             (CO4)

U_f(a) = 6a f'(a)-2(1-a^2)f''(a)
         -2(1-a^2)f'(a)^2-ra,
```

and U_g has the analogous formula with b. At every interior pair a,b,
the complete local-energy range along its configuration fiber has
width

```text
2 sqrt((1-a^2)(1-b^2)) |f'(a)g'(b)|.                  (CO5)
```

If R were constant on the entire carrier, CO1 and CO4 would force
f'(a)g'(b)=0 for all interior a,b. Either f' vanishes everywhere or,
choosing any a where it does not vanish, g' vanishes everywhere. Hence
one of f,g is constant. The potential term for that plaquette remains
-ra or -rb, and cannot be canceled by the other single-variable terms.
At r != 0 this contradicts constant local energy. Thus the complete
exact-vacuum fiber within this class is NONE.

An exact witness for the first-order trial f(a)=ra/6, g(b)=rb/6 uses

```text
configuration I:   P=i sigma_1,  Q= i sigma_1; a=b=0, c=-1;
configuration II:  P=i sigma_1,  Q=-i sigma_1; a=b=0, c=+1.
```

Both are realized by the link assignment after CO1. Their local energies
are respectively

```text
R_I = -r^2/12,       R_II = -5r^2/36,
|R_I-R_II| = r^2/18.                                  (CO6)
```

Indeed Gamma(a+b,a+b)=8+2c at a=b=0 and the accepted trial residual is
-r^2 Gamma(a+b,a+b)/72. The common-edge gradients cancel in the first
configuration and reinforce in the second. All separate plaquette
traces agree, while the requested residual does not.

## 4. A stronger theorem: arbitrary dependence on only a,b also fails

**Theorem.** Suppose a smooth function on the full link carrier has the
form psi(U)=Psi(a(U),b(U)), with Psi smooth on (-1,1)^2. If
A(r)psi=E psi for a constant E and r != 0, then psi is identically zero.
In particular, the actual positive vacuum cannot have this form.

**Proof.** The chain rule, CO2, CO3, Ta=6a and Tb=6b give

```text
A(r)Psi = 6a Psi_a+6b Psi_b
          -2(1-a^2)Psi_aa-2(1-b^2)Psi_bb
          -(c-ab)Psi_ab-r(a+b)Psi.                    (CO7)
```

Fix any interior a,b. All terms except -(c-ab)Psi_ab are independent
of c, while CO1 supplies a nondegenerate interval of actual c values.
The eigenfunction equation therefore forces Psi_ab=0 everywhere on
(-1,1)^2. Integrating this identity on the rectangle gives

```text
Psi(a,b)=h(a)+j(b).
```

Substitute this expression into CO7. Differentiate the resulting
identity once in a and once in b. The separate kinetic terms and the
right side E(h+j) have zero mixed derivative. The potential term gives

```text
-r[h'(a)+j'(b)]=0.
```

Since r != 0 and the two variables vary independently, h'=k and j'=-k
for a constant k. Thus Psi=C+k(a-b). Its eigenfunction equation is

```text
6k(a-b)-rC(a+b)-rk(a^2-b^2)=EC+Ek(a-b).                (CO8)
```

The coefficient of a^2 forces k=0. The coefficient of a then forces
C=0. Hence Psi vanishes on the interior trace image. That image is
dense in the full configuration space; smoothness, or just continuity,
extends the equality to its boundary. This proves the theorem. No
positivity assumption was needed for the obstruction. QED.

For an arbitrary log candidate F(a,b), the coefficient of -(c-ab) in
its local energy is F_ab+F_a F_b. This is exactly Psi_ab/Psi when
Psi=exp(F). Canceling this single coefficient is necessary but does not
solve the remaining eigenfunction equation, as the theorem establishes.

## 5. A quantitative fiber obstruction for approximate candidates

There is an exact residual lower bound as well. Under product Haar,
the two holonomies P,Q are independent Haar elements. To see this,
condition on e: A and B are independent Haar products of disjoint
exterior links, and left/right translation preserves Haar measure.
Their conditional joint distribution is independent of e.

For interior a,b, the two quaternion axes are independent uniform
unit vectors. After fixing one axis, the dot product with the other
is uniform on [-1,1] by the sphere's area element. Therefore c is
uniform on I(a,b), giving

```text
E_mu[c-ab | a,b]=0,
E_mu[(c-ab)^2 | a,b]=(1-a^2)(1-b^2)/3.                (CO9)
```

For any smooth real Psi(a,b), and any real E, CO7 separates the
unnormalized eigenfunction residual into

```text
(A(r)-E)Psi = D_E(a,b)-(c-ab)Psi_ab,
```

where D_E is the sum of the terms in CO7 other than the cross term,
minus E Psi. Squaring and integrating the exact conditional identity
CO9 yields

```text
||(A(r)-E)Psi||_2^2
 = E_mu[D_E(a,b)^2]
   +(1/3) E_mu[(1-a^2)(1-b^2)Psi_ab(a,b)^2].           (CO10)
```

Thus the second term is a compulsory squared-residual contribution
for any approximation in this compressed class. If Psi is positive
and normalized, its tilted measure Psi^2 dmu still has a uniform c
fiber at fixed a,b; the analogous pointwise local-energy variance is

```text
Var[R | a,b]
 = ((1-a^2)(1-b^2)/3) (Psi_ab/Psi)^2.                (CO11)
```

These formulas do not prove that the total residual has a uniform
strict lower bound over all normalized candidates in this class.
They isolate the precise lost contribution and retain the separate
D_E obligation when Psi_ab is small or zero.

## 6. Next retained quantity, locality boundary, and evidence

The representation (a,b) fails the kinetic receiver already on the
single product observable ab:

```text
T(ab)=13ab-c.                                         (CO12)
```

This follows directly from T(ab)=a Tb+b Ta-Gamma(a,b).
Adding c, or the equivalent shared-edge cross gradient, is necessary
to preserve this operation. Its geometrical support is the outer six
links. The full cross term c-ab also contains the product ab, which
uses all seven links. The next construction must preserve this joint
dependence instead of keeping only separate trace values.

This is not a theorem that every possible nearest-neighbor conditional
construction fails. Full link conditionals may retain group-valued
staples and other joint data, and an exact law on this two-square
graph can use the new trace. The proved exclusion is precisely the
global class psi=Psi(a,b). It does not establish a finite-range
obstruction for unrestricted larger-graph conditional laws.

Two boundary controls limit the conclusion. At r=0, the constant
function is an exact vacuum, so the nonzero-coupling condition cannot
be dropped. For edge-disjoint squares joined only by bridges,
Gamma(a,b)=0; the single-square positive vacua multiply to an exact
vacuum with constant bridge dependence. This follows by separating
the kinetic terms and the two plaquette potentials. The shared edge
is a load-bearing premise of the obstruction.

Evidence grade: written proofs and explicit exact SU(2)
configurations. The identities cover all group values, all spins,
and the stated gauge sector on this fixed graph. They are not a
numerical sample, a formal proof assistant certificate, a larger
coupling-window theorem, or a volume-uniform collective estimate.
