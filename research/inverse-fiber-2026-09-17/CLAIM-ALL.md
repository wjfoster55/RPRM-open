# IF-a: every Boolean extra on two named two-point fibers

17 September 2026. Rank-7 all-function cut. Nulls are frozen in
[NULL-ALL.md](NULL-ALL.md) and were written before this cut's
enumerator ran.

## Task record

1. **Carrier, types, equality, admitted context.** XOR=1 and C=(0,0)
   as two-point fibers. Boolean readouts are all total maps to `{0,1}`
   on Bits2 (16) and Bits3 (256). Truth-table equality. Linear and
   affine maps are a typed subset, not the declared menu.
2. **Supplied ports, missing ports, requested readout.** Supplied: the
   two fibers and the complete function tables. Missing: which tables
   split each fiber to ONE. Readout is the complete splitting family
   (listed for 16; counted and criterion-checked for 256) plus whether
   that family equals the linear duals of the fiber direction.
3. **Operation kind, direction, enabledness.** Observation. Direction
   is refinement of an already-complete fiber. Enabledness is total.
4. **Receiver.** Remaining source identity after one extra bit. A
   constant-on-fiber readout leaves MANY.
5. **Inverse / complete fiber.** Split-to-ONE iff `D` is injective on
   `F`. On these fibers that is `D(p)≠D(q)`. `NONE`/`ONE`/`MANY` of a
   refined fiber is not re-asked; the asked fiber is the family of
   splitting `D`.
6. **Coverage, hostile case, evidence grade.** Coverage: all 16 and
   all 256. Hostile: constant-on-fiber extras; “only a coordinate or
   parity”; “exactly the linear duals.” Grade: **finite exhaustive
   test**. Not Lean. Not a physics law.

## Claim

On these two-point fibers, an extra Boolean readout splits to ONE
exactly when it takes different values on the two members. That is
8 of 16 tables on XOR=1 and 128 of 256 on C=(0,0). The linear duals
of the fiber direction are a proper subset. 2-bit parity does not
split XOR=1. A constant-on-fiber extra is not discriminating.

## What this is not

Not IF-v's named-menu census. Not a theorem for fibers larger than
two points. Not a claim that the useful views are exactly the linear
duals. Not a 4-bit lift. Not a physics law.
