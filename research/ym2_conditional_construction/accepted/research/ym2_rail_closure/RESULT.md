## A precise version of being determined through the surroundings

For every link of a finite SU(2) lattice, supply its proposed conditional
probability density given all the other links. These are full conditional
laws, not a claim that the exact quantum vacuum depends only on immediately
adjacent links. Changing one link gives a ratio of density values.

Now change two links. There are two routes: change the first and then the
second, or the second and then the first. Both must give the same total
ratio. The [conditional reconstruction proof](CONDITIONAL_RAIL.md) shows
that if **every such rectangle closes**, positive smooth conditional laws
on the compact product determine one unique normalized joint law.

Why is that enough? Any finite update path can be rearranged by swapping
adjacent changes of different links. The rectangle rule preserves its
ratio. Repeated changes to the same link cancel or telescope. What remains
depends only on the endpoints. An arbitrary reference configuration fixes
an initial scale; normalization removes it. Thus the route is dispensable
for this readout once the conditional laws and their proof remain available.

This is a useful, exact meaning of proceeding without a chronological
ledger. It does not mean proceeding without retained information. Nor
does it make the whole configuration uniquely determined: one unique
probability law can give positive probability to many possible states.
Obtaining and checking arbitrary conditional laws may still be expensive.

The necessity has an exact small hostile case. Two individually positive,
normalized bit rules give ratio 1 along one route from 00 to 11 and ratio
2 along the other. There is no joint positive law satisfying both. The
failure is in their relationship, even though each rule looks valid alone.

The probability-theory reconstruction ingredient is established. Besag's
1972 primary treatment derives joint density ratios from conditional
specifications and their consistency restrictions. The present note
supplies the compact smooth SU(2) proof and the following explicit vacuum
and gap adapter; no literature priority claim is made.
[Besag, section 2](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/GibbsFieldEst/Besag1972.pdf).

## Two more checks prevent a consistent network from certifying itself

Let p be the reconstructed density and phi its positive square root.
For the retained Hamiltonian A=T-rS, the first further question is whether
applying A to phi changes it by the **same scalar everywhere**:

```text
(A phi)(x)/phi(x) = E, independent of x.
```

This can be evaluated directly from derivatives of the supplied conditional
laws. The proof derives the formula, including the SU(2) metric and factors.
If it is constant, the ground-state form identity proves phi is the actual
positive vacuum of this Hamiltonian. If it varies, the conditional network
has reconstructed a different law.

We have an exact SU(2) separator already available: the explicit reference
phi proportional to exp(rS/6). Its conditionals are perfectly compatible.
On one square its local energy is -r²(1-a²)/18. At plaquette trace a=1 it
is zero; at a=0 it is -r²/18. For r>0 those differ, so consistency alone
cannot promote this reference to the actual vacuum.

The last check is collective response. Perturbing one surrounding link
changes another link's conditional law. Put rigorous upper bounds on
these responses into a nonnegative matrix B. A sufficient certificate is

```text
B s <= q s,     s_i > 0,     q < 1,
```

with a graph-independent strict margin, plus a common finite one-link
gradient Poincare constant over every exterior. Then the accepted forest
cover converts those controls into a uniform physical gap bound.

The weights s allow different sites to be measured with different scales.
This is a useful realization of the user's changing-lens idea: the ratios
are adjusted to the relations being bounded, rather than imposing one
equal weight everywhere. A three-site exact example has ordinary maximum
row sum 6/5, which fails, but passes the weighted test with q=9/10.
The [chain note](CHAIN_ESTIMATES.md) proves the improvement. This example
is a comparison matrix, not a measured influence matrix for the YM vacuum.
Weights cannot fix a genuinely noncontracting collective mode.

## Why a closed rail can still contain a slow point

Think of many people linked by elastic bands. Each person's local movement
may be well controlled while the group can sway together very slowly.
Relations can be internally consistent throughout that sway. Internal
agreement and resistance to a collective movement are different questions.

The new four-state calculation makes this exact. Take reference laws
proportional to exp(a x y), with x,y in {-1,+1}. Their heat-bath gap is
exactly 1-tanh(a). Along the loop

```text
a:   0 -> log 2 -> log 4 -> log 2 -> 0
gap: 1 ->  2/5  -> 2/17  ->  2/5  -> 1.
```

Every reference change has an exact inverse, and the final reference is
the initial reference. Nevertheless the middle is slower. Subdividing
the loop into smaller valid steps does not remove that slow point.

The chain note goes further using the accepted smooth SU(2) mixture
family. It constructs exact closed loops with arbitrarily small
one-link **and mixed-response increments**, while the middle reference
diffusion has gap at most 2/[N m(t)^2], tending to zero with graph size.
That auxiliary law is not asserted to be the actual YM vacuum. It is an
exact obstruction to a general inference from valid small handoffs to
a uniform gap.

There is no contradiction with changing coordinates. Transport the full
fixed Hamiltonian, including its residual, and its spectrum is unchanged.
The slow-point examples vary the reference diffusion itself. Forgetting
that residual would change the physical question being measured.

## What cancellation buys, and what still needs proof

For exact references phi_k and the same actual vacuum psi, write
v_k=log(psi/phi_k) and h_k=log(phi_(k+1)/phi_k). Then

```text
v_n = v_0 - sum h_k,
2 log phi_n + 2v_n = 2 log psi.
```

The reference movement cancels before taking absolute values. This is
where the signed-relations intuition supplies a concrete improvement:
estimating the two moving pieces separately can lose the cancellation
that the physical density actually has.

For approximate updates the new note derives the exact error formula
delta_n=delta_0-sum e_k. A small error at each step is insufficient on an
arbitrarily long route. A bounded signed partial sum, a bounded primitive,
or an independently proved contracting correction would suffice. The
new exact control keeps every signed prefix at most 1/10 although adding
the absolute increments gives 10. Equal-signed increments really do grow.
Cancellation is therefore a property to derive and retain, not assume.

The next mathematical obligation is now more specific. Construct actual
conditional kernels on a larger coupling domain and prove together:

1. Every coordinate rectangle closes and gauge covariance holds.
2. Their derivative formula gives constant local energy for the retained A.
3. Their one-link constants and weighted collective response have uniform
   bounds over all configurations and admitted graph sizes.

Alternatively, a controlled approximate family can work if its correction
or residual is bounded strongly enough in those same local and mixed
readouts. An average L2 bound alone does not supply that control. This
conditional route bypasses a particular global Fourier construction as
a formulation; it does not bypass the nonlinear vacuum equation.

The existing actual strong-coupling gap bound is unchanged. We have not
constructed the needed larger-domain kernels or extended its coupling
window. In the accepted lattice convention r=8/g_b^4, the weak-bare-coupling
continuum route moves toward r growing without bound, outside that window.
Changing energy units cannot alter this dimensionless ratio. The present
work therefore sharpens a research route toward the mass-gap ambition;
it supplies no continuum quantum Yang–Mills mass-gap proof.
