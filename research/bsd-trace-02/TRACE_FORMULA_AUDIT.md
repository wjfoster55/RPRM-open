# Exact CM orbit and trace audit for the p=5 experiment

This note proves a replacement for constructing the degree-256 polynomial
and audits the subsequently executed trace. The computed Gaussian-unit
multiple is **100 modulo125**, so its 5-adic valuation is exactly two.
The finite action certificate is `work/cm_action.py`, with fresh output
`evidence/cm_action.json`; the independent arithmetic check is
`work/trace_formula_reassembly.py` and its matching evidence file.

The carrier is K=Q(i), O_K=Z[i], and the fixed curve is
E:y²=x³−1156x with CM action [i](X,Y)=(−X,iY). Points are compared as
elliptic-curve points, including O. The requested readout is the 5-adic
valuation of the specified critical Hecke value. A Gaussian unit ambiguity
is harmless for this readout, but would not identify a signed rational
critical value. The finite CM orbit below is complete; a trace calculation
has its own separate certificate and is not inferred from the orbit size.

## Primary inputs and the square case

The attributed dependency `../bsd-identity-01/ODD_PRIME_ATTEMPT.md` fixes
E':y²=x³+289x, the CM-compatible K-isomorphism with E, and the conductor
f=68Z[i]. For D'=−289, the odd-D clause of [CLS I, Lemma 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.3832#page=13)
applies. The direct even-D substitution for D=1156 is not used.

The CM inputs are the Frobenius action ψ(w) on division points, its norm
and trace at split good primes, and the identification of K(E_f) with the
ray class field of modulus f. Relevant primary statements are [CLS II,
equations (7)–(8)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.4206#page=4), [CLS I,
Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.3832#page=13), and its [Frobenius trace
discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.3832#page=17).
Thus [K(E_f):K]=φ(f)/4. At a split rational good prime ℓ, the two
Frobenius values are a±bi with 2a=a_ℓ and a²+b²=ℓ.

[CLS II, Lemmas 4.1 and 4.4 and equation (77)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.4206#page=15)
require a fourth-power-free D divisible by an odd prime. They do not
require that prime to have odd valuation. Their proofs use the conductor,
ray fields, and the nontrivial involution; they do not invoke CLS I's
different odd-valuation lemma. Hence D'=−17² is admitted. With
f1=34(1−i), their degree is φ(f)/8=256. The corrected formula gives
c5_plus(E)=±16T/68⁵ for T=Tr_(H_f1/K)(2ρ⁵+289ρ), as in the dependency.

These are theorem inputs. The following action and algebra calculations
are independently written and finitely checked here.

## Recovering the entire arithmetic action from two primes

Let A=(O_K/68)×. The CRT gives

    |A|=|(O_K/4)×|·|(O_K/(4+i))×|·|(O_K/(4−i))×|
       =8·16·16=2048.

The actual Galois image on E[68] has size 2048/4=512. Its action is
faithful because K(E[68]) is the field generated by all those points.
E[68] is a free rank-one O_K/68 module.

The new program counts points using square multiplicities, without using
CM predictions as the counting algorithm:

| ℓ | #E(F_ℓ) | a_ℓ | the two Gaussian Frobenius values |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 5 | 8 | −2 | −1+2i, −1−2i |
| 13 | 20 | −6 | −3+2i, −3−2i |

Both primes are good, split, and prime to 68. For each pair there is no
remaining sign choice in the real part; conjugating i only interchanges
the two elements, both of which are used. Each generated action therefore
belongs to the actual Galois image.

Multiplication of pairs is

    (a,b)(c,d)=(ac−bd,ad+bc) mod68.

Exact enumeration gives a subgroup G of size 256 after the first pair,
and 512 after adding the second pair. Inclusion in the actual image and
equality of finite cardinalities prove that G is the entire image.
This is stronger than guessing a factor or orbit from its degree.
The program separately checks closure and inverses for the resulting set.

Let μ4={1,−1,i,−i}. The same finite certificate checks

    G ∩ μ4={1},             μ4 G=A.

Consequently every primitive 68-division point is [u][g]R0 for exactly
one u∈μ4 and g∈G, once a reference primitive point R0 is fixed.
The program also verifies that −1+2i has order 16 modulo 68.

## The necessary primitive-point certificate

The factorization is (68)=(1+i)^4(4+i)(4−i), up to a unit. A point R
is a generator of E[68] as an O_K/68 module exactly when

    [68]R=O,
    [34−34i]R≠O,
    [16−4i]R≠O,
    [16+4i]R≠O.                                      (1)

The three nonzero conditions test f divided by each distinct Gaussian
prime factor. In a cyclic module over each prime-power factor, they say
that the component has the maximum allowed order. The CRT then proves
the assertion jointly. Integer order 68 by itself is insufficient:
it does not distinguish the two split factors of 17.

For a point satisfying (1), the 512 points [g]R are all distinct and form
its full arithmetic orbit. A direct distinctness check in a computation
is useful confirmation, but the module argument explains its force.

## A rational function removes the square-root choices

Write B=289 and define on E

    r(R)=Y/(2X),             h(R)=2r(R)^5+B r(R).

The poles of r are O and T0=(0,0); neither occurs in a primitive orbit.
The dual rational 2-isogeny E→E' is

    X'=(X−4B/X)/4,
    Y'=Y(1+4B/X²)/8.

Substituting Y²=X³−4BX proves

    X'=r(R)²,             pullback(ω_E')=2ω_E.        (2)

For the reference complex point R0 with parameter Ωplus(E)/68,
the image parameter is 2Ωplus(E)/68=Ωplus(E')/68. This is precisely
the argument defining ρ in the admitted formula. Hence r(R0)=±ρ;
the real sign does not enter the valuation readout.

There is also an explicit involution fixing r. Addition by T0 gives

    X(R+T0)=−4B/X(R),       Y(R+T0)=4B Y(R)/X(R)²,
    r(R+T0)=−r(R),          r(−R)=−r(R).

Thus r(−R+T0)=r(R). The element

    j=f1−1=33−34i mod68

is in G, is not 1, and has square 1; all three facts are checked in
`cm_action.py`. For every primitive R, [f1]R is the unique nonzero
point of E[1+i], namely T0. Therefore [j]R=−R+T0. This proves directly
that r is fixed by a nontrivial arithmetic involution.

The relation

    X²−4r²X−4B=0,          Y=2rX

shows that [K(R):K(r)]≤2. Since j fixes r but moves primitive R,
the degree is exactly 2. Thus [K(r):K]=256 and

    S(R):=(1/2) Σ_(g∈G) h([g]R)
         =Tr_(K(r)/K)(2r⁵+B r).                      (3)

The factor 1/2 removes the proven twofold multiplicity. It is not an
empirical normalization. Comparing (2) with the specified ρ identifies
S(R0)=±T.

For u∈μ4 the given CM action yields r([u]R)=u^(-1)r(R).
Because 5≡1 mod4, both terms in h transform by the same factor:

    h([u]R)=u^(-1)h(R),       S([u]R)=u^(-1)S(R).

Every primitive R is a unit times a Galois conjugate of R0, so

    S(R)=ε T,        ε∈μ4,        v_5(S(R))=v_5(T).   (4)

This proves why an arbitrary **certified primitive point** is sufficient
for this valuation experiment. An arbitrary polynomial factor without
the action and primitive-point certificate is not sufficient.

## Computing the same trace modulo 125

Good reduction at 5 and gcd(5,68)=1 make E[68] finite étale locally at 5.
Its reduction lifts uniquely through the unramified extension to each
modulus 5^k. Since Frobenius 5 has order 16 on this module, all the
68-division points occur over F_(5^16). Either choice of the split
embedding i in Z_5 conjugates the Gaussian action and leaves the group
certificate and valuation criterion unchanged.

A computation can therefore:

1. certify its residue field and its chosen lift ring modulo 125;
2. construct R and check (1), first in the field and then on its torsion lift;
3. evaluate [a+bi]R=[a]R+[b]([i]R) for the 512 pairs in `cm_group()`;
4. evaluate (3) with modular inverses only of certified units;
5. check that the result lies in the embedded base ring and test its residue.

Primitive torsion does not reduce to either pole of r, so X and 2 are
units at every evaluation point. Intermediate group-law denominators
still require correct exceptional-case handling. A point on the curve
modulo125 is not enough: the lifted point must be the 68-torsion lift.
Likewise, simply reducing an arbitrary characteristic-zero trace into a
field does not justify division or a chosen lift without these checks.

The trace is locally integral. Under either split embedding of K at 5,
Gaussian units remain units in Z/125. Consequently (4) gives the exact
equivalence

    S(R) mod125 ∈ {25,50,75,100}  <=>  v_5(T)=2.      (5)

The set in (5) is preserved by multiplying by any unit modulo125. The
certificate need not recognize the complex sign, the correct Gaussian
unit, or any predicted Sha value. A residue outside that set does not by
itself prove nontrivial Sha; the cited criterion is sufficient, not a
general converse.

## Executed result, Hensel audit, and both primes over five

The new `evidence/trace125.json` has SHA-256
`e061991f753dd3d136a658cf3fdb79ae2aa60e164a04c8bc15036d2b984c8a66`.
Its production-source hash is
`f1f9eb8039d9088f0d0e1e318771b880c58e3578c0e7457e55e2ab641c12699a`.
The source audit and independent reassembly refer to these bytes.

The field certificate uses the correct degree-16 Rabin criterion:
z^(5^16)=z and gcd(z^16−2,z^(5^8)−z)=1. Two is the only prime dividing
16. Hence the residue algebra is a field. Its defining derivative is a
unit at every root, giving the unramified lift algebra modulo125.

The production Hensel procedure treats x as a local parameter at the
primitive seed and solves y²=x³−1156x on the branch determined by its
residue y. Here 2y is a unit. Its dual-number derivative is along that
curve branch, with dy/dx=(3x²−1156)/(2y). It then solves the ordinate
of [34]P equal to zero; the computed derivative is a unit. The recorded
successive orders are at least 1, 2, and 3. After each x correction the
curve ordinate is corrected again. In addition to these Newton checks,
the final point is checked on the curve, with its original residue,
[68]P=O, all three tests (1), and [34]P one of the three explicit nonzero
2-torsion points. Those final identities certify the prime-to-five
torsion lift independently of trusting the history of the iterations.

The separate reassembly script imports neither `trace125.py` nor its
field class. It uses direct wrapped polynomial multiplication and unit
inversion by the exponent 25(5^16−1), instead of the production inverse
algorithm. It independently verifies the final point, residue, [34]P,
[68]P, and all three CM primitive tests. It reconstructs the action
group and recomputes every one of the 512 supplied fifth-power terms.
It checks the 256 distinct r values with multiplicity two, the identity
and involution evaluations, and the resulting scalar

    S=100 mod125,          S/25=4 mod5.

The evidence states its exact scope: it does not independently derive
all 512 r coordinates from the point. The production source audit
checks that step; a separately implemented orbit calculation is an
additional complementary check, not silently part of this reassembly.
No source or receipt hash is treated as a mathematical proof.

Finally, this valuation statement covers both primes above five. The
distinguished T belongs to Q, since the corrected critical-value formula
has rational value and real rational scaling factor. Equation (4) says
the computed S is εT with ε a Gaussian unit. Every Gaussian unit has
valuation zero at each prime above five. Valuation two at the computed
embedding therefore proves v_5(T)=2 for the rational T, and hence the
same valuation at the other embedding. Recognizing the particular ε is
unnecessary and has not been claimed.

The factor 16/68⁵ is a five-adic unit, so v_5(c5_plus(E))=2. The admitted
curve has algebraic and analytic rank two, good split reduction at five,
#E(F5)=8, and the required prime-to-six and period-unit conditions.
[CLS I, Theorem 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.3832#page=5) therefore
gives Sha(E/K)[5^infinity]=0. Restriction followed by corestriction is
multiplication by [K:Q]=2; it is invertible on the 5-primary subgroup.
Thus restriction is injective there and

    Sha(E/Q)[5^infinity]=0.

This is one primary component. It does not establish full Sha finiteness,
the complex BSD identity, or rationality of its central-derivative
quotient. The critical fifth-power Hecke value remains a different
analytic quantity from L''(E,1)/2.

## Coverage and evidence grade

The group generator certificate is an exact finite computation with
explicit CM and class-field theorem inputs. The displayed isogeny,
involution, degree, and unit-ambiguity arguments are written proofs.
They do not become formal proofs because their arithmetic is executable.
The modular point/trace arithmetic has its own executed certificate and
the independent checks described above. This note closes the
orbit-selection, normalization, and primewise theorem-application
questions for those certificates; it does not imply the complex BSD
identity. No formal proof assistant verification was run.
