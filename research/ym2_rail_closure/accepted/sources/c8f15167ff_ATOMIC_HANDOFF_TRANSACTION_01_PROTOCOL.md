## Question

Can the already programmed local release be encoded as one transaction over an
immutable pre-state, with fold, flick, and scoop emitted only as three views of
one commit? A successful transaction must:

1. debit the old internal register and credit the successor environmental
   register together;
2. give all three receipt faces one transaction identity and one carry packet;
3. count the material transfer once and the relation as an edge, not another
   conserved object;
4. leave zero unassigned transaction delta and preserve the six-unit ledger;
5. publish nothing and leave the pre-state unchanged under injected failure;
6. feed the committed state directly into the next native tick without reset.

This is a software transaction/refinement question. It is not a test of
quantum collapse, the Born rule, DNA, or physical simultaneity.

## Why this follows the causal-state fold

The `/2` audit showed that internal reconciliation was not a sufficient
prospective handoff rule: it occurred 653 times with release in the five prior
positives, but also 1,201 times without release in the seven negatives. This
protocol keeps reconciliation as one gate and adds the rest of the installed
valve evaluation before commit. That closes an implementation hole; it does
not discover a new natural completion law.

Prospective eligibility is true only when the immutable boundary state and
agitation satisfy every frozen gate:

- agitation is present;
- the carrier is at the released address;
- carrier payload and environmental stock are reconciled;
- the center boundary is closed;
- the precommit ledger closes;
- the relation law is enabled;
- the frozen local cascade plans a receiver registration and positive lawful
  transfer.

The planner never reads the observed commit, the source success label, the
history label, or a post-result classification. Because it directly evaluates
the programmed valve, success is evidence for prospective planning and
transaction integrity only.

## Atomic contract

The formal interface is:

```text
HANDOFF(post-reconciliation state, agitation)
  -> next ONE receipt
   + carry packet receipt
   + sealed old-state/exhaust receipt
   + zero unassigned transaction delta
```

The output receipts are non-additive views. The ledger counts the transfer
packet exactly once. “Zero unresolved remainder” means the planned delta is
fully assigned between source debit and receiver credit; it does not mean the
physical exhaust profile is empty.

The public trace has only precommit and committed states. Fold, flick, and
scoop receipts share one transaction ID, one pre-state hash, one post-state
hash, and one carry-packet hash. Receipt publication order is projected in all
six permutations and may not change the state or receipt set.

## Iterations and controls

The single admitted execution runs the twelve frozen histories for each of the
twelve frozen subjects for 48 native ticks. Within that execution it performs
these sealed iterations:

1. healthy atomic commit and shared three-face receipt identity;
2. all six receipt-order permutations;
3. atomic failure injection at three would-be staged boundaries;
4. a deliberately observable `fold -> flick -> scoop` staged reference;
5. direct successor continuation with no reset or new state schema;
6. a relation-severed control plus all seven prior-negative subjects.

The staged reference reserves a material buffer, debits the source, credits the
receiver, and clears the buffer as separate visible operations. Its healthy
final state must equal the atomic final state. An interruption after buffer
creation, source debit, or receiver credit-before-clear must expose a buffer or
an incomplete ledger. This comparator is intentionally uncompensated; logging,
locking, or compensation can make real staged systems safe.

Atomic injected failures occur before publication. Every one must retain the
exact pre-state hash, publish zero receipts, expose zero intermediate states,
and require no compensating transaction.

## Claim boundary

Even the strongest result says only that one atomic transaction is a coherent,
fault-contained encoding of the frozen toy valve and removes the need for a
public intermediate buffer. It does not show that the valve is naturally
emergent, that all staged implementations are invalid, that “there is no
three” is an ontological fact, or that nature implements this transaction.
