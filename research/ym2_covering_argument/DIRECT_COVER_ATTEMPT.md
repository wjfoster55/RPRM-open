# Direct Fourier-norm construction and all-state gap bound

12 September 2026. This is a new written proof with two independent
agent reviews of its load-bearing analytic steps:
[direct review](DIRECT_COVER_REVIEW.md) and
[second review](DIRECT_COVER_RPRM_REVIEW.md).
It does not modify the accepted two-square or connected-vacuum packets.
Its analytic estimates are derived below; no simulation, spin cutoff,
old verification suite, or theorem-checker execution is used.

**Result.** Let `G` be a finite
open square or cubic lattice graph, with at least one link, full link
Hilbert space, distinct elementary square plaquettes, and

```text
Q_G = SU(2)^(E_G),                   d in {2,3},
T = -(1/2) sum_(e,k) D_(e,k)^2,      D_(e,k) generated by i sigma_k,
S = sum_p a_p,                      a_p = (1/2) Tr(U_p),
A(r) = T-rS,                        m = 2(d-1).
```

The product metric makes each `SU(2)` a unit round `S^3`. All spins are
admitted and equality in the Hilbert space is almost-everywhere equality
for product normalized Haar measure. `A(r)` has the usual compact-source
Laplacian domain; the real bounded potential preserves that domain.

The argument gives the explicit sufficient interval and bound

```text
0 <= r <= 1/(48m)   ==>   gap(A(r)) >= 1/2.                 (DC1)
```

The constants are independent of link and plaquette counts. They are
deliberately conservative. Thus `r<=1/96` in dimension two and `r<=1/192`
in dimension three suffice. The same bound holds on the gauge-invariant
subspace with gauge transformations imposed at every vertex, whenever
that subspace has a nonzero excited complement. In a tree-only physical
sector with no boundary charges the invariant Hilbert space is only the
vacuum line; then the corresponding form inequality below is vacuous on
the orthogonal complement, rather than a claim that an excited eigenvalue
exists. For the
original energy scale, `H=alpha hbar^2[A(r)+r N_p]`, this gives
`gap(H)>=alpha hbar^2/2` when `r=beta/(alpha hbar^2)` is in this interval.

The receiver is the complete orthogonal complement of the actual ground
state, including arbitrarily high spins. The output is a lower spectral
bound and a convergent construction of that ground state. It is not a
complete enumeration of interacting eigenfunctions. No continuum scaling
or infinite-volume Hilbert-space construction is claimed.

## 1. A local norm on the full Peter--Weyl expansion

For a multi-spin `J=(j_e)`, let `pi_J(U)=tensor_e pi_(j_e)(U_e)` and

```text
supp J = {e : j_e>0},
lambda_(e,J) = 2 j_e(j_e+1),
lambda_J = sum_e lambda_(e,J).
```

The `J=0` coefficient is the Haar mean. Write each isotypic component
uniquely as

```text
u_J(U) = Tr(B_J pi_J(U)),      b_J = ||B_J||_1,
```

where `||.||_1` is the matrix trace norm. In the usual convention,
`B_J` is the dimension factor times the Fourier coefficient, with any
transpose determined by the displayed trace convention. Define the
unweighted compact-group Fourier norm and the local energy norm by

```text
||u||_A = sum_J b_J,
||u||_* = max_(e in E_G) sum_(J:e in supp J) lambda_J b_J,
X_G = {u : B_0=0 and ||u||_*<infinity}.                    (DC2)
```

`X_G` is a Banach space. For fixed finite `E_G`, its norm is equivalent
to the weighted sum `sum_(J!=0) lambda_J b_J`: the maximum in (DC2) is
at most that sum, while the sum is at most `|E_G| ||u||_*`. Completeness
therefore follows from completeness of the weighted direct sum of finite
matrix spaces. Volume-dependent equivalence constants are used only for
fixed-graph existence and regularity, never in the estimates below.

These are ordinary Fourier-algebra coefficients, not a representation
cutoff. The compact Fourier algebra is a Banach algebra under pointwise
multiplication; see [Eymard's original paper](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/bsmf.1607/).
The particular inequality needed here also has a finite-dimensional proof.
For two individual components, with `d_K=||C_K||_1`,

```text
u_J v_K = Tr[(B_J tensor C_K)(pi_J tensor pi_K)].
```

Decompose the tensor-product representation unitarily into irreducibles.
Deleting its off-diagonal blocks is a trace-norm contraction (pinching),
and summing the multiplicity-diagonal blocks is partial trace, whose
trace-norm contraction follows by duality against `I tensor M` with
`||M||<=1`. Hence

```text
sum_L ||(u_J v_K)_L||_A <= b_J d_K,
supp L subset supp J union supp K                        (DC3)
```

for every reached component `L`. Support can shrink by cancellation of
representations; this is allowed and retained. If both input components
are trivial at one link, every output component is trivial there. Thus
(DC3) gives the needed support bound without requiring equality of
supports. Approximation by finite sums extends the product estimate to
absolutely summable expansions.

## 2. The inverse kinetic operator cancels the output energy weight

Put `Qf=f-integral f dmu` and define `T^-1` on mean-zero functions by
multiplication of component `J!=0` by `lambda_J^-1`. The zero component
is removed before applying this inverse.

For a unit tangent generator at link `i`, the spin-`j_i` representation
derivative has operator norm `2j_i`. Indeed it is unitarily conjugate to
the diagonal generator with weights `2m`, `m=-j_i,...,j_i`. Set

```text
c_(i,J)=2j_i,       c_(i,J) <= (2/3) lambda_(i,J).          (DC4)
```

The inequality holds for every nonzero half-integral spin since
`c/lambda=1/(j+1)<=2/3`, and both sides vanish at spin zero. In particular,
`||D_(i,k) u_J||_A<=c_(i,J)b_J` for each of the three generators.

For the bilinear operation

```text
B(u,v)=T^-1 Q sum_(i,k) (D_(i,k)u)(D_(i,k)v),
```

we claim

```text
||B(u,v)||_* <= (8/3) ||u||_* ||v||_*.                    (DC5)
```

To prove it, fix an output anchor `e`. The factor `lambda_L` in (DC2)
cancels `lambda_L^-1` from `T^-1`; removal of the scalar component can
only decrease the remaining sum. By (DC3), an output supported at `e`
has `e in supp J union supp K`. Bounding this indicator by the sum of
the two input indicators safely double-counts the case where both contain
`e`. For the first indicator, (DC3)-(DC4) give

```text
sum_(J:e in supp J) b_J
  sum_i 3 c_(i,J) sum_(K:i in supp K) c_(i,K)d_K
<= sum_(J:e in supp J) b_J
  sum_i 3 c_(i,J) [(2/3)||v||_*]
<= (4/3) ||v||_* sum_(J:e in supp J) lambda_J b_J
<= (4/3) ||u||_* ||v||_*.
```

The second indicator gives the same bound with `u,v` reversed. Their
sum proves (DC5). Derivatives force shared support at the differentiated
link; edge-disjoint input supports contribute zero before any counting.

## 3. The plaquette source has a bounded anchored norm

Each plaquette half trace expands into `2^4=16` products of four
fundamental matrix entries, each with coefficient `1/2`. An entry of a
unitary irreducible representation is a rank-one coefficient with
Fourier norm one. An inverse entry is a conjugated/transposed entry of
the dual representation, again with norm one. Because each of the four
links is a distinct occurrence, the norm of each product is one by
the tensor-product coefficient formula. Therefore

```text
||a_p||_A <= 8.                                          (DC6)
```

This upper bound suffices; equality is not needed. Every term has spin
`1/2` on precisely the four plaquette links and zero elsewhere, so
`T a_p=6a_p`. Even where coefficients add in a common block, the triangle
inequality gives

```text
||T^-1 S||_* <= 8 max_e #{p:e in p} <= 8m.                (DC7)
```

No plaquette count `N_p` occurs in (DC7). At most `m=2(d-1)` elementary
plaquettes contain a fixed link on an ordinary open lattice.

## 4. A convergent fixed point for the actual vacuum

Seek the real log wavefunction `u` with Haar mean zero. Define

```text
F_r(u)=r T^-1 S+(1/2)B(u,u).                             (DC8)
```

For `R=1/4`, (DC5)-(DC7) imply on the closed radius-`R` ball

```text
||F_r(u)||_* <= 8mr+(4/3)R^2,
||F_r(u)-F_r(v)||_* <= (8/3)R ||u-v||_*.
```

When `0<=r<=1/(48m)`, the first upper bound is
`1/6+1/12=1/4=R`; the second multiplier is `2/3<1`.
Thus Banach contraction gives a unique fixed point in this ball, with

```text
||u||_* <= 1/4.                                         (DC9)
```

Iteration from zero converges in `X_G`, and every step is real and gauge
invariant. Reality follows because (DC8) preserves real functions; gauge
invariance follows because `T`, `Q`, and the kinetic gradient pairing
commute with the full vertex gauge action. Alternatively, gauge invariance
of the final positive vacuum follows from uniqueness below.

For completeness, the sharper scalar majorant gives

```text
R_-(r)=(3/8)[1-sqrt(1-128mr/3)],
||u||_*<=R_-(r),             0<=r<3/(128m).               (DC10)
```

The closed ball of radius `R_-` maps to itself and has contraction
multiplier `(8/3)R_-<1`. At `r=0`, the fixed point is zero. Equation
(DC1) uses the simpler closed interval from (DC9), so no endpoint
contraction ambiguity enters its stated bound.

For each fixed finite graph, `sum_J lambda_J b_J<infinity` implies
uniform convergence of `u` and all first and second left-invariant
derivatives. For example, products of two generator norms are bounded
by a fixed constant times `lambda_J` by Cauchy--Schwarz and
`(2j)^2<=2[2j(j+1)]`. Thus `u` is `C^2`. Applying `T` to (DC8) gives

```text
T u = rS+(1/2)|grad u|^2+E,
E=-(1/2) integral |grad u|^2 dmu,                         (DC11)
```

using `integral S=0`. Finite-dimensional elliptic regularity then
bootstraps the equation to smoothness: the initial `C^2` regularity
makes the right side `C^1`, hence locally Holder; Schauder improvement
and iteration yield every finite derivative order. Constants in this
regularity step may depend on the finite graph, which does not affect
the uniform norm or Hessian estimates.

The positive function `psi=exp(u)` satisfies exactly

```text
(T-rS) psi=E psi.                                       (DC12)
```

This retains the extensive vacuum shift. It has not set `E` to zero or
assumed the original constant vector remains the vacuum. Define
`psi_hat=psi/||psi||_2`. The ground-state form identity (DC14), obtained
directly from (DC12), proves that `A(r)-E` is nonnegative and its null
space is exactly the span of `psi_hat`: a zero form value requires
`grad f=0`, hence `f` constant on the connected product manifold. Thus
`E` is the bottom eigenvalue and is simple. This argument uses only the
constructed positive function and does not need a separate positivity
improvement theorem. Adding a constant to `u` has no effect on these
statements or the following estimates.

## 5. A Hessian bound with no hidden connection term

Fix any point and any tangent vector `v=(v_e)`, where `v_e` belongs to
the three-dimensional tangent space of link `e`. Extend it by the
constant left-invariant field `V=sum_e V_e`. For a bi-invariant metric,
`nabla_V V=(1/2)[V,V]=0`, including the product factors. Consequently

```text
Hess u(v,v)=V^2u
```

at the chosen point. This diagonal quadratic-form computation includes
the Levi-Civita connection; it does not replace the possibly nonsymmetric
matrix `D_a D_b u` entry by entry with the Hessian.

On representation `J`, the generator in direction `v` has operator
norm at most `sum_e 2j_e |v_e|`. Cauchy--Schwarz with weights
`lambda_(e,J)` gives

```text
(sum_e 2j_e |v_e|)^2
<= lambda_J sum_(e in supp J) [(2j_e)^2/lambda_(e,J)] |v_e|^2
<= 2 lambda_J sum_(e in supp J) |v_e|^2.
```

After summing trace norms and interchanging nonnegative sums,

```text
|Hess u(v,v)|
<= 2 sum_e |v_e|^2 sum_(J:e in supp J) lambda_J b_J
<= 2 ||u||_* |v|^2.                                    (DC13)
```

This holds at every source configuration and every tangent vector,
including vectors supported on arbitrarily many links.

## 6. All-state covering by the weighted Bochner identity

Let `dnu=psi_hat^2 dmu`, where `psi_hat` is the normalized positive
function defined after (DC12). The ground-state transform `Uf=psi_hat f`
is unitary from `L^2(nu)` to `L^2(mu)`. For each finite graph,
`psi_hat` and its reciprocal are smooth bounded multipliers with bounded
first derivatives, so they map the inherited `H^1` form domains onto
one another. Its transported operator and form are

```text
U^-1(A(r)-E)U=-(1/2)L,
L=Delta+2 grad u dot grad,
<Uf,(A(r)-E)Uf>=(1/2) integral |grad f|^2 dnu.             (DC14)
```

The product of unit round three-spheres has `Ric=2g`, independently of
the number of factors. Its weighted Ricci tensor for `nu` is

```text
Ric_nu=Ric-Hess(log dnu/dmu)=2g-2 Hess u
       >= [2-4||u||_*]g.                                (DC15)
```

The weighted Bochner identity, with this normalization of `L`, is

```text
(1/2)L|grad f|^2-<grad f,grad Lf>
  = |Hess f|^2+Ric_nu(grad f,grad f).
```

Integration against its invariant probability measure `nu` gives

```text
integral (Lf)^2 dnu
 = integral |Hess f|^2 dnu+integral Ric_nu(grad f,grad f) dnu
 >= kappa integral |grad f|^2 dnu,
kappa=2-4||u||_*.
```

For any nonconstant eigenfunction `-Lf=ell f`, integration by parts
yields `ell^2||f||_2^2>=kappa ell||f||_2^2`, hence `ell>=kappa`.
The smooth compact weighted manifold has compact resolvent. Its full
eigenbasis and form closure therefore give the Poincare inequality
for every function in the form domain:

```text
integral |grad f|^2 dnu >= kappa Var_nu(f).                (DC16)
```

This explicitly covers every spectral component. Complex functions
follow by applying the real inequality to their real and imaginary
parts. It is the familiar Bakry--Emery argument, with its spectral
step reproduced here so the required receiver is visible.

Combining (DC9), (DC14), and (DC16) proves

```text
<phi,(A(r)-E)phi>
 >= (1/2)[||phi||_2^2-|<psi_hat,phi>|^2]                   (DC17)
```

for every `phi` in the full form domain. This is (DC1). More generally,
(DC10) gives `gap>=1-2R_-(r)>1/4` throughout its strict interval.
The lower bound is not sharp at zero: the actual full-link free gap is
`3/2`, whereas this Ricci-only argument supplies `1` at zero.

Gauge invariance does not remove the ground state: each gauge
transformation commutes with `A(r)` and preserves its positive normalized
ground state by uniqueness. Restricting (DC17) to invariant functions
therefore proves the same gap lower bound for the physical subspace.
No tensor-product structure of that constrained subspace is assumed.

## 7. Hostile controls and claim boundary

The undressed excitation-number shortcut fails even on one square.
Let `Omega=1`, `a` be its half trace, and `N_exc=sum_e (I-P_e)` where
`P_e` projects a link onto its constant function. For the unnormalized
real vector `z_t=1+t a`, Haar orthogonality and the four fundamental
link labels give exactly

```text
<z_t,(-r a)z_t>=-rt/2,
<z_t,N_exc z_t>=t^2,
<z_t,T z_t>=(3/2)t^2.
```

Here `integral a^2=1/4` and `integral a^3=0`. Thus no bound
`|<z,(-ra)z>|<=C r <z,N_exc z>` can hold for every `z` with fixed
finite `C`: choose positive `t<1/(2C)`. The missing linear coupling
to the vacuum cannot be discarded. Equations (DC11)-(DC14) treat it
by constructing the actual vacuum and subtracting its actual energy.

The norm must also retain its energy weight. Without it, arbitrarily
large spin gives generator norm `2j` and second derivative norm of
order `j^2` at fixed coefficient trace norm; neither (DC5) nor (DC13)
would follow from the unweighted anchored norm alone.

Duplicating plaquette occurrences changes the incidence port: `m` must
then count them with multiplicity. Using `m=2(d-1)` after duplication
would be invalid. Changing the generator metric changes both `lambda`
and `Ric`; the displayed numbers cannot be copied unchanged. The proof
uses distinct four-link square plaquettes and the stated source metric.

The construction handles a bounded weak-magnetic interval at the fixed
lattice energy scale. The usual weak-bare-coupling continuum direction
has growing magnetic/electric ratio and is outside this interval. An
infinite-volume state, its representation, convergence of observables,
renormalization, and a positive gap in physical continuum units remain
separate obligations. No statement here closes those fibers.
