### A naive shift changes no majorant feasibility sign

For the existing scalar majorant

`M_x(Z)=25*x^2/16 + 4*x*Z/3 + Z^2/6`, with `L_x(Z)=(4*x+Z)/3`,

an equation-preserving proportional shift gives

`M_alpha=(M_x+alpha*Z)/(1+alpha)` and `L_alpha=(L_x+alpha)/(1+alpha)`.

Therefore `Z-M_alpha=(Z-M_x)/(1+alpha)` and `1-L_alpha=(1-L_x)/(1+alpha)`. For `alpha>=0`, an invalid sign stays invalid. ### Compact SAT closure depends on what each message can express

Two compact CNF chains imposing opposite parities are jointly inconsistent. Any boundary cube leaving a bit free contains both parities. A certificate restricted to covering the boundary by block-forbidden cubes therefore needs `2^b` singleton pieces. Keeping the parity equations instead closes this family with a short contradiction.

But changing everything to flat unions of affine pieces also fails a size test: for `t` disjoint clauses `(x_i or y_i or z_i)`, the whole relation has `7^t` points and each contained affine piece has at most `4^t`, requiring at least `ceil((7/4)^t)` pieces. The original factored formula is small and easy to decide. Thus the receiver and composition grammar matter, not merely whether each operation is exact. [PNP proof and scope](agents/PNP_AUDIT.md)

## What “digitally safe” still needs to mean

The digit alphabet and the operation are different ports. In a base-ten chart, the label 5 can become 6 under the specified shadow composition. That identity does not make 5 a universal safe endpoint. We need a predicate `Safe(state, context, receiver)` with a proof that either:

1. the requested answer is already certified there; or

2. a specified continuation remains enabled and preserves all information needed for that answer.

Zero must retain its role as a value, a placeholder, or a lost column according to the particular operation. Dropping it without that distinction can merge incompatible states. Changing radix can change a digit-based predicate; if the proposed conclusion is intrinsic, an adapter must show the conclusion agrees under the corresponding change of representation. The present carry theorem works for every supplied base, but that does not establish an arbitrary safe-digit rule.
