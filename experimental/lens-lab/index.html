Behind the play

Keep the relation.
Change the view.

The source is five ordered points on a 360-step circle. We keep one anchor and each point’s offset from it. Moving the anchor rebuilds all five points while preserving their relative angles.

Each lens gives those same points a different job: hue, wave offset, or position. Its own controls change that mapping. The composition combines all three.

Try breaking the lock—and see what a view can forget

Move one: turn off “Move together,” then drag a point. Its relationships change; the other four points stay put.

Flatten a view: set curve Height or geometry Spread to zero. Different points now land on the same output. The source still keeps their offsets, so raising the control brings them back.

The exact rule: point[i] = (anchor + offset[i]) mod 360. Retaining the anchor and ordered offsets reconstructs the tuple. Offsets alone leave 360 possible anchors. Colors and screen pixels are display adapters; they are not an inverse for the source.
